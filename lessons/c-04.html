Adjustments: what you may and may not do

8 minutes · Frames, styling and measuring

Purpose

You know which frame adjustments a Level 1 team member may do, which need a qualified colleague, and when to stop. The aim is a comfortable pair for the patient and no broken frames.

Worked example

Mr T, 70, retired, comes in because his glasses slip down his nose. They are an acetate pair. You look first: the sides are a little loose behind the ears, and the pads are not part of the frame because it is plastic.

You do not reach for the pliers. You ask yourself three questions.

  1. What is it made of? Acetate. It can be warmed gently, but only with a proper frame warmer, never a lighter or a hairdryer on full heat.
  2. Could I repair it if it breaks? You cannot repair a snapped acetate side. So you check with a qualified colleague, and say so: "It is an older frame, so I would rather ask first."
  3. Is it a job for a side bend or something else? The tips are the loose part, so a small bend of the earpiece will help.

Your colleague warms the earpiece in the warmer, you bend the tip down a little to follow the ear, and you check the fit again on Mr T. He looks down and shakes his head. The glasses stay. You tell him: "Come back any time. It costs nothing to check the fit."

The lesson

What a Level 1 may do (with a model shown by a coach)

  • Clean the lenses and frame.
  • Tighten loose screws at the hinge with the correct screwdriver, but check the screw is not stripped.
  • Adjust nose pads on metal frames: move them in or out, or alter their angle a little. Use pad pliers.
  • Straighten the sides by gentle hand pressure on metal frames.
  • Make small bends to the earpiece (the bent tip) on metal frames using the right pliers.
  • Warm and gently bend the sides or earpieces of acetate frames using a proper frame warmer, following the training you have been given.
  • Check the final fit with the patient in front of you.

What needs a qualified colleague

  • Any adjustment you have not been trained on.
  • Bending a front, or twisting the bridge of a plastic or metal frame.
  • Rimless or three-piece frames, where a hard knock to a mount or a drilled hole could crack the lens.
  • Frames made of titanium, memory metal, wood, horn, carbon fibre or injected plastic, which react differently to heat and force.
  • Any frame with a lens in it that is very strong or thick, or that the patient has just bought.
  • Repairs: soldering, replacing hinges, re-glazing.
  • Anything where the patient says the frame has already been repaired.

The golden rule: never adjust a frame that you could not repair if it broke without asking. If an old frame snaps in your hands, the patient is the one who loses. Ask first. Say what you are going to do, and what might happen.

How to adjust safely

  1. Look first: what is it made of, how old is it, has it been adjusted before?
  2. Use the right tool: pad pliers for pads, side bending pliers for sides, a proper warmer for acetate.
  3. Make a small change, then re-check the fit on the patient. Small steps.
  4. Never force a bend. If there is resistance, stop.
  5. Warm acetate evenly and gently. Overheated acetate goes shiny or bubbles.
  6. Check both sides are equal before the patient leaves.

Watch-outs

  • Do not adjust a pair in front of a patient if you are not confident. Say: "I am going to ask a colleague to help here."
  • Never use a naked flame or hot air gun.
  • Do not bend a frame with lenses fitted without first checking the lenses will not pop or crack.
  • A frame that keeps slipping or pinching after two adjustments may be the wrong frame.
  • If unsure, ask a registrant or a qualified colleague.

Tips

  • Practise on spare or display frames with a coach.
  • Take pliers to the patient's chair so you adjust with them in front of you.
  • Tell the patient what you are doing and why: it builds trust.

Current practice notes

  • Who may do which adjustments is a practice decision. Check with your practice manager: your practice's list of adjustments a Level 1 may do unsupervised.
  • Frame warmers and pliers are kept in the dispensing area. Check with your practice manager where.

Drill

A patient hands you a thin, old metal frame with a small crack near the hinge and asks you to "tighten it up". Say out loud what you do.

Show model answer

Stop. Do not adjust it. A cracked old frame could snap and you could not repair it. Ask a qualified colleague, explain the risk to the patient honestly, and offer a repair by the lab or a replacement frame if it cannot be done safely. Never adjust a frame that you could not repair if it broke without asking.

Today on the floor

Show your coach where on a spare frame you would adjust nose pads or sides. Name two jobs you must pass to a qualified colleague.

About 4 minutes, with your coach.

What your coach looks for

  • Level 1 adjustment named correctly
  • Hands on cracked, rimless or titanium frames
  • Uses a spare frame, not a patient's glasses
