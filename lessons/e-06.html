Remakes and warranty jobs in Optix

5 minutes · Systems

Your manager shows you this one. Read it for the idea; your manager shows you the exact steps on screen in your practice.

Purpose

After this lesson you know what to record when a pair has to be remade and what you must not promise. A calm, accurate record protects the patient, the practice and the supplier claim.

Worked example

Mrs D, 62, bookkeeper, returns a week after collection and says her new varifocals "do not feel right".

  1. Listen and ask what she notices. Write it down in her words.
  2. Note the date of purchase and the original order reference.
  3. Do not say "we will remake them". Say, "I will ask someone to look at this properly."
  4. Pass her to a registrant for assessment, as your practice does.
  5. Record the reason for the return in Optix One (desktop) and the original order reference.

Nothing was promised, and the facts were recorded before the decision.

The lesson

What is known.

  1. Record the reason for the remake or warranty return in the patient's record.
  2. Record the original order reference.
  3. Never promise a free remake before the problem has been triaged. A remake might be a warranty matter, a supplier error, an adjustment, or neither.
  4. Keep the original glasses unless told otherwise.
  5. Keep the patient informed. Tell them when you will get back to them and do.
  6. A registrant looks at any clinical complaint.

What is not yet written. Who decides a free remake, how a supplier claim is raised, and how the job is recorded in Optix One (desktop) (a new order, a linked order, a note).

Process to be confirmed by: practice manager (with the owner for warranty wording). Known so far: record the reason, the original order reference, and never promise a free remake before triage.

Eyecoded lenses. The group is moving to eyecode all eligible lenses. Any warranty advantage of eyecode is Check with your manager ; do not promise it.

Tone. Be warm and calm. The patient is not a problem; the pair is.

Watch-outs

  • Promising a free remake on the spot.
  • Throwing away the original pair.
  • Skipping the record of the reason.
  • Blaming the patient or the lab in front of the patient.

Tips

  • Ask open questions: "What do you notice?"
  • Write the date and the order reference at the top of the note.
  • Agree when you will call the patient back, then call.

Drill

A patient says, "These are rubbish, remake them free." Write two sentences you would say that promise nothing but show you are acting.

Show model answer

"I am sorry they are not right. I will note exactly what you are noticing and ask the right person to look at it, and I will call you by (day)." You promise a callback, not a remake.

Today on the floor

Ask a colleague who knows the practice's process: 'If a pair needs remaking, who decides if it is free and what do I record?' Then role-play the first minute with a colleague who plays a patient asking for a free remake. Nothing real is recorded; practise only what you would say.

About 4 minutes, with a colleague.

What your coach looks for

  • Takes details and gives a time to ring back
  • Does not promise a free remake or a refund
  • Says what must be recorded: reason and original order reference
  • Stays warm and calm
