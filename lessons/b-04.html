Office lenses: Varilux Immersia MID (our E-reader)

7 minutes · Lenses

Purpose

After this lesson you can explain what our office lens is for, who it suits, and who it does not suit, and you can use the right product name when ordering. It matters because a patient who wears their varifocals at the desk all day often ends up craning their neck, and a second pair solves it.

Worked example

Mrs H, 59, an office manager, wears varifocals and says, "At work I tilt my head back to see my screen and my neck aches by lunchtime. My screen is in the middle of the varifocal, where it is narrow."

You ask her how far her screen is (about 70 cm), whether she looks at papers on the desk, and whether she also walks about the office. She looks at the screen and papers all day and only gets up for meetings.

You explain: "Your varifocals have to do everything, so the part for the screen is quite narrow. An office lens is made for the desk: it gives a wide, comfortable view from reading distance out to about the other side of the room. It is not for walking, driving or stairs, so it is a second pair that lives at work." She asks whether she could use it everywhere. You say no: the far distance is limited, so keep the varifocals for everything else.

She chooses the office lens in a frame she is happy to leave at work. You note on the dispense that it is a desk pair only.

The lesson

An office lens (an occupational progressive) is a progressive lens designed for the short distances: close, arm's length and a room. It has a gentle power change that covers reading distance up to a few metres. Because it does not try to serve the far distance, the zone for close and middle vision is much wider than in a standard varifocal.

Our office lens is Varilux Immersia MID. In Optix and on the price list it appears as the E-reader. When you order, you order Immersia MID, not Digitime; we do not order Digitime. Essilor describes Immersia MID as a lens for people who work at a desk and in a room, with a wide near and middle zone.

Who it suits: anyone with a near add who spends long periods at a desk or screen, or reading, such as an office worker, a teacher at a whiteboard, a musician at a stand, a craftsperson at a bench. It is also a good second pair for a varifocal wearer whose neck aches.

Who it does not suit: anyone who needs the pair for driving, walking about, stairs or outdoor use. It is not a replacement for distance vision. A person who needs far vision at work (for example, a lecturer looking to the back of a hall) may need a different answer; ask a registrant.

Adaptation: like any progressive, it takes a short time to learn where to look. Tell the patient to keep the head still and let their eyes find the clear area. Book a check if they find it hard.

Eyecoding and personalisation: Immersia MID is ordered with VisiOffice measurements where possible. Measurements for the working distance matter, so ask the patient how far away their screen is and write it down.

Price and bands: Immersia MID is on the price list as E-reader, by index band. For example, at the standard index the clear E-reader pair is £245, with the Gold member price at £196. Not every Transitions combination is offered; the Transitions E-reader exists only in the thinnest band, so check price-it before offering it.

Watch-outs

  • Never sell an office lens as the only pair for a driver.
  • Do not use the words "reading glasses" for an office lens: it is not a pair for walking about.
  • Do not order Digitime. Order Varilux Immersia MID.
  • Do not promise a Transitions option without checking that the band offers it.
  • If unsure whether the patient needs far vision at work, ask a registrant.

Tips

  • Ask "how far away is your screen and what is on your desk?" and write the answer on the dispense.
  • Tell the patient the pair "lives at work" so they do not wear it outside.
  • Offer it as a second pair rather than a replacement.

Current practice notes

  • E-reader on the price list means Varilux Immersia MID; staff order Immersia MID, not Digitime.
  • Immersia MID Transitions is only offered in the thinnest band on the group price list; check price-it.
  • Essilor describes Immersia MID as a lens for desk and room distances.

Drill

A "Mr L, 63, a piano teacher" wears varifocals and has neck ache looking at his music stand about 80 cm away. In 30 seconds, tell him what you would offer, what it is not for, and what you will write on the dispense.

Show model answer

Offer an office lens (E-reader, Varilux Immersia MID) as a second pair for the music stand, because it gives a wide view at that distance. It is not for driving, stairs or walking about, so he keeps the varifocals for those. On the dispense write that it is a desk/stand pair, the working distance (80 cm), and that VisiOffice was done or why not.

Today on the floor

Find the office lens in the lens list. Explain to a colleague who it suits and who it does not, and what you would write on the order.

About 4 minutes, with a colleague.
