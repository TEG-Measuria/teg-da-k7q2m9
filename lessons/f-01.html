The 8-step collection routine

8 minutes · Aftercare

Purpose

You can run a collection in eight steps, so the glasses are right, the patient leaves confident and nothing is missed. A good collection is where the sale is won or lost for the next pair.

Worked example

Mr D, 52, plumber, is collecting his first varifocals. Before he sits down you open his job in Optix One (desktop) and put the glasses next to the order: right frame, right lens type, right tint, right colour. They match. You then pre-adjust the frame on the bench so it is roughly right before it touches his face.

He sits. You fit the glasses, check they sit level and that the nose pads and sides are comfortable. You ask him to look across the room, read the notice on the wall, then hold his phone at a normal distance. Then you ask him to turn his head a little while looking at the door. "It feels odd at the edges," he says. You tell him that is normal for a first varifocal and give him the adaptation script. He says, "They look a bit heavy." You agree that new glasses feel different and ask him what he likes about the frame. You book the follow-up call for a week's time. Last, before the glasses leave your hand, you check his full name and date of birth against the job.

The lesson

The collection routine has eight steps. Do them in this order every time.

  1. Check the job against the order first. Do this before the patient sits down. Is it the right patient, frame, lens design, material and coating? A mistake found now costs you five minutes; a mistake found at home costs a remake and a patient's trust.
  2. Pre-adjust. Get the frame roughly right on the bench: the sides level, the pads symmetrical, the angles about right.
  3. Fit. Put the glasses on, check the sit and the comfort, and make the fine adjustments with the patient looking at you.
  4. Check distance, near and in motion. Ask the patient to look across the room, to read something close up, and to move their head and walk a few steps. Watch their face as well as listening to their words.
  5. Give the care and adaptation script. Cover cleaning, the case, wearing the glasses all day starting first thing in the morning, and what to expect (see the next lesson).
  6. Deal with first-impression objections. "They feel heavy", "the frame looks different" and "it feels strange" are very common. Listen, agree it is a change, and reassure; do not argue.
  7. Book the follow-up. Use a follow-up call for a first varifocal or anyone unsure. Put the date in the diary before the patient leaves.
  8. Check two identifiers before you hand the glasses over: the full name and one other, such as date of birth or address.

Why this order? Step 1 comes first because every later step wastes time if the glasses are wrong. Step 8 comes last because it is the final gate.

Level 1 does collections alone after five observed collections. Until then, a coach watches you and signs each one off. If at any step you are unsure what you are looking at, stop and ask a colleague or a registrant.

Watch-outs

  • Do not skip step 1 because the job "looks right". Compare the glasses to the order every time.
  • Do not make fine adjustments while the patient is looking down at their phone. Check vision first, then adjust.
  • If the patient reports double vision, or says the glasses are blurred or hurt their head straight away, stop and fetch a registrant.
  • Do not hand over glasses until two identifiers match.

Tips

  • Say what you are doing as you do it: "I am just checking these against your order."
  • Praise the choice the patient made; it helps them feel good about the glasses.
  • Keep the cleaning kit and the case in your hand ready to give out at step 5.

Current practice notes

  • Level 1 does collections alone after five observed collections.
  • Practices use a 20-minute dispensing or fitting slot for collections.
  • Check with your manager whether the record of observed collections is kept on paper or in Optix One (desktop). Check with your practice manager.

Drill

Say the eight steps out loud in order, then tell a colleague what you would do if the frame in the bag is not the one on the order.

Show model answer

1 check the job against the order; 2 pre-adjust; 3 fit; 4 distance, near and in motion; 5 care and adaptation script; 6 first-impression objections; 7 book the follow-up; 8 check two identifiers. If the frame is wrong, stop at step 1, do not fit it, tell the practice manager or dispensing optician and phone the patient before they come in.

Today on the floor

Role-play a collection with a colleague playing the patient, using the eight collection steps. Use a stock or demo pair, not a real job. The colleague ticks each step on the Level 1 collection checklist as you do it and notes any starred step you missed.

About 5 minutes, with a colleague.

What your coach looks for

  • Checks the job against the order before the patient sits down
  • Pre-adjusts, then fits with the patient in a mirror
  • Asks the patient to look far, near and move
  • Books the follow-up and checks two identifiers last
