The care and adaptation talk

7 minutes · Aftercare

Purpose

You can give a short, clear talk at collection that tells the patient how to look after their glasses and what to expect in the first weeks. It prevents most unnecessary return visits.

Worked example

Mrs P, 66, retired teacher, is collecting her first pair of varifocals. After the fit and the checks, you say: "Here is your cleaning kit. Use the spray and the cloth, not your sleeve or a tissue. When they are off, they go in the case, not on the dashboard. And one more important thing: wear them first thing tomorrow morning and keep them on all day, rather than swapping between these and your old pair. Your eyes and brain need a few days to get used to them, and it can take a couple of weeks to settle. If they have not settled in that time, or if something worries you, ring us and we will see you."

Mrs P asks, "What if the stairs feel funny?" You tell her to look down through the lower part of the lens, move her head rather than her eyes, and take the stairs slowly the first few times. You write the follow-up call in the diary.

The lesson

The care and adaptation talk is step 5 of the collection routine. It has two halves.

Care

  • Clean with the spray and the cloth supplied, using both hands on the frame.
  • Keep the glasses in the case whenever they are off.
  • Do not rest them lens-down, and do not leave them in a hot car.
  • Come back for adjustments any time; they are expected.

Adaptation

  • Start wearing the new glasses first thing in the morning, not halfway through the day.
  • Wear them as much as possible; swapping back to the old pair slows adaptation.
  • Adjusting can take a few weeks. Ask the patient to call if things have not settled in a couple of weeks.
  • For varifocals, tell the patient to point their nose at what they want to see, and to use the lower part of the lens for near.

What is normal at first A feeling of unfamiliarity, a slight awareness of the frame on the nose or ears, and a little difficulty with stairs or kerbs for the first days with a varifocal are normal.

When to ring us the same day Double vision, a headache that will not ease, feeling sick, or a sudden loss of sight are not "getting used to it". Ask the patient to ring or return straight away. You do not diagnose; you pass the call to a registrant (see the next lesson).

Say it in your own words. Do not recite it like a leaflet. Watch the patient's face; if they look worried, slow down and ask what they are thinking. Always end by asking, "Is anywhere tight or uncomfortable?" and tell them you are always here for adjustments.

Watch-outs

  • Do not promise that adaptation "will be fine". Say it usually settles, and invite them back.
  • Do not hand over a leaflet and leave it at that: say it out loud.
  • Do not tell a patient to "just put up with" double vision or a bad headache. Pass it to a registrant.

Tips

  • Teach the three-word varifocal rule: nose at target.
  • Offer to write the call-back date on the case sleeve or the leaflet.
  • A second pair for work or driving is still worth mentioning; adapt to the main pair first.

Current practice notes

  • Patients are advised to start new glasses first thing in the morning and to call if things have not settled in a couple of weeks.
  • Practices offer a follow-up call after collection; first varifocals are always booked for one.
  • Check with your manager whether the six-month complimentary mini-service is still offered. Check with your practice manager.

Drill

In 30 seconds, give the care and adaptation talk to a colleague, then tell them one symptom that means "ring us the same day".

Show model answer

Cover: cleaning kit, case, wear first thing in the morning and keep them on, expect a few weeks, call if not settled in a couple of weeks. Same-day symptoms: double vision, a headache that will not ease, feeling sick or sudden loss of sight.

Today on the floor

Give the care and adaptation talk to a colleague in your own words, in under two minutes. They tick each point from the lesson and tell you which part sounded like reading a leaflet.

About 3 minutes, with a colleague.

What your coach looks for

  • Covers cleaning, the case, wearing them all day from the morning, and not swapping back
  • Says what is normal at first and when to ring the same day
  • Ends by asking if anywhere is tight or uncomfortable
  • Does not diagnose; passes concerns on
