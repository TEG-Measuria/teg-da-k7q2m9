Eyezen Start and Eyezen Boost for screen users

8 minutes · Lenses

Purpose

After this lesson you can recognise a patient who may suit Eyezen Start or Eyezen Boost, explain the difference in plain words, and remember that both must be eyecoded. It matters because heavy screen users are one of our biggest groups of patients, and a good conversation here prevents "my eyes feel tired" returns.

Worked example

Mr J, 38, an accountant, says, "I am on two screens for nine hours. By the afternoon my eyes feel tired and I get a bit of a headache. My distance vision is fine, I just have a mild prescription." The optometrist has found nothing wrong with his eyes and has advised glasses.

You ask: how far are the screens, how long at a stretch, and does he also use his phone a lot? He says a desk screen at about arm's length, a laptop, and his phone all evening.

You explain: "These glasses are single vision lenses with a design made for people who look at screens. They have a little extra help in the lower part for near work, so your eyes may not need to work as hard on close and phone distance. Many people say they feel more comfortable at the end of the day." You make no health promise: you describe comfort, not a cure. You check his understanding ("Does that sound like your day?"), then show him the price on the price list and note on the dispense that he is a heavy screen user.

The lesson

Eyezen is a family of Essilor single vision lenses designed for people who spend a lot of time on screens. Essilor describes the range as lenses with extra support for focusing at close and middle distances, so the wearer may feel more comfortable at the end of a long screen day. We offer two:

  • Eyezen Start is the entry design in the family. It suits a patient who does a lot of screen work but whose near focusing is still comfortable, often younger wearers.
  • Eyezen Boost adds more help for close work and suits a patient who reports tired eyes, difficulty with close work or a longer day on devices. The strength of the boost depends on the lens chosen.

How to choose between them: ask about the day and about comfort, then follow the practice's guidance. If the patient is close to needing a reading prescription, or the optometrist has noted a near add, the conversation changes and you should ask a registrant, because the right answer may be a varifocal or an office lens (b-04).

The key points for the patient, in plain words:

  1. It is a single vision lens with a design made for devices.
  2. It is for comfort, with no promise about eye health.
  3. It comes with the group's standard Crizal UV coating (see b-11).
  4. It can be clear or, in some bands, Transitions (see b-12). Not every combination is offered, so check price-it before promising.

Both designs must be eyecoded. Eyecoding means the lens is made using the patient's own measurements from VisiOffice, so the lens position and design suit that wearer. It is our house rule that every Essilor patient has VisiOffice measurements. Practical steps: take the VisiOffice measurements, import them into the Essilor ordering site (Opsysweb), and make sure the Eyecode tick is on in the patient section. If VisiOffice cannot be done, note why on the dispense and use the average values in the fallback card. Never press SEND yourself; only a trained person sends the order.

For the cost, show the patient the price for their index band. For example, Eyezen Start clear at the standard index is £235 a pair and Eyezen Boost clear at the same index is £250. The Gold member prices are £188 and £200. Always check the price list for the band that matches the patient's prescription.

Watch-outs

  • Do not say blue light causes eye damage. Eyezen is a comfort conversation.
  • Do not use Eyezen as a substitute for a reading or varifocal prescription. If the patient has a near add, ask a registrant.
  • Do not promise a lens combination before checking the price list; some combinations are not available.
  • Do not skip VisiOffice. If you cannot do it, write why on the dispense.

Tips

  • Ask the patient to describe their longest screen day.
  • Put the screen distance on the dispense in centimetres.
  • Offer a trial-style phrase: "Many people notice they are more comfortable by the end of the day; if you are not happy, we have a process to put it right."

Current practice notes

  • Eyezen Start and Eyezen Boost must be eyecoded; TEG rule is VisiOffice on every Essilor patient.
  • Essilor describes Eyezen as lenses for digital device users; exact boost values and age guidance are in Essilor's product guide.
  • Prices are per pair by index band and clear or Transitions on the group price list.

Drill

Out loud, in 30 seconds, explain Eyezen to "Ms F, 44, a call-centre worker who is on screen all day and has tired eyes by 3pm".

Show model answer

"These are single vision lenses with a design made for people on screens. They give a little extra help for close work, so many people find the end of the day more comfortable. It is about comfort, not a treatment. We will take your measurements on our equipment so the lens is made for your eyes and your frame." Then ask whether she also needs help with reading, and if so, ask a registrant.

Today on the floor

Pretend a colleague works all day on a screen and has tired eyes by mid-afternoon. Explain Eyezen in 30 seconds.
