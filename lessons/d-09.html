NHS vouchers: who is eligible

7 minutes · Pricing

Purpose

After this lesson you can recognise the main groups of people who may be entitled to an NHS optical voucher and know what to ask next. You do not decide eligibility alone: you collect the evidence and, if unsure, ask.

Worked example

Mrs W, 33, shop assistant, says she gets "help with health costs" and asks for a voucher for her new glasses.

  1. Ask what she receives and ask to see the evidence (for example her benefit letter or her NHS certificate).
  2. Do not assume. "Help with costs" can mean different things.
  3. Check the evidence is current and in her name.
  4. If it clearly shows entitlement, carry on to the voucher steps. If it does not, or you are unsure, ask your practice manager before promising a voucher.

You collect and check. The paperwork decides, not what sounds likely.

The lesson

What an NHS optical voucher is. A voucher is a contribution towards the cost of glasses or contact lenses for people the NHS says are entitled. It is not a free pair of glasses. It is a fixed amount that depends on the lens needed.

Who may be entitled (in outline). The public NHS rules say a voucher may be available to:

  • children under 16
  • people aged 16, 17 or 18 in full-time education
  • people who receive certain income-related benefits, or who are part of a household that does
  • people holding an NHS certificate for help with health costs (a full or partial help certificate)
  • people whose prescription is complex, because of its strength or because it includes prism

Different from the eye test. Entitlement to a free NHS eye test is a separate question with a wider list of people, such as people over 60 or with certain eye conditions. Being eligible for a free eye test does not mean the person gets a voucher.

What you do.

  1. Ask the patient why they think they are entitled.
  2. Check the evidence the practice accepts.
  3. Check the details are in date and in their name.
  4. Record what you saw, as your practice asks.
  5. If you cannot tell, ask your practice manager.

What you do not do. You do not guess eligibility. You do not issue a voucher on a patient's word alone. You do not ignore a doubt because the patient is in a hurry.

Under 16 and sight impaired. These patients must be dispensed by or under the supervision of a registrant. A voucher does not change that.

Values. The voucher value depends on the lens. Do not quote voucher values from memory or old posters: values are read from Optix One (desktop) or the current guidance.

Watch-outs

  • Assuming eligibility from a patient's story.
  • Mixing up free-eye-test entitlement and voucher entitlement.
  • Quoting a voucher value from memory.
  • Dispensing for an under-16 without a registrant.

Tips

  • Ask politely: "Can I see your certificate, please? I need to note it down."
  • Keep a quick list of accepted evidence beside the till.
  • If someone is short of paperwork, offer to hold the job until they return with it.

Current practice notes

  • Public NHS rules on who may be entitled to an optical voucher, summarised in outline.
  • Check with your manager the exact current eligibility list and voucher values against the DHSC 2026 letter and Optix One (desktop)'s voucher set-up. Check with your practice manager.
  • Under-16s and registered sight impaired patients are dispensed by or under a registrant.

Drill

A patient aged 70 says, "I get free eye tests, so I get a free pair of glasses with a voucher." What do you say?

Show model answer

"Free eye tests and vouchers are two separate things. A free eye test does not on its own give a voucher. Let me check what you may be entitled to." You then ask about other grounds and ask your practice manager if unsure.

Today on the floor

Ask your coach for a made-up person aged 70. Say what you ask next about NHS voucher eligibility. No real patient involved.

About 3 minutes, with your coach.

What your coach looks for

  • Does not decide eligibility alone
  • Asks for the right evidence
  • Does not say free glasses
