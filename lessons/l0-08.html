Learning from shadowing: your 20 dispenses

8 minutes · Induction

Purpose

After this lesson you know how to get the most from your first 20 shadowed dispenses and what observed sign-off will look like later. It matters because watching well is the fastest way to learn what the lessons cannot show.

Worked example

Mr B, 24, is on his third day. He is shadowing a Level 3 colleague who is dispensing glasses for Mrs S, 66, a retired nurse. He has a small notebook. He writes: "Asked what Mrs S does in a day. Asked about the old glasses first. Chose the frame before the lenses. Said the price after explaining why. Checked the job against the order at collection. Asked for name and date of birth." He writes no patient name. After the patient leaves he asks one question: "Why did you ask about the old pair first?" The answer teaches him more than a page of text.

The lesson

The target. At Level 0 you shadow at least 20 dispenses. You do not serve alone. Count them in a notebook with no patient names.

What to watch. Pick one theme per dispense so that you are not trying to see everything at once.

  • The greeting and how the dispenser puts the patient at ease.
  • Needs-finding: what questions are asked about work, screens, driving, hobbies and the old glasses.
  • Linking a lens to a need, and saying the lens before the price.
  • Good, better, best, and how the price is stated.
  • The second-pair and sun conversation.
  • How measurements are taken.
  • How the dispenser decides to fetch a registrant.

The 8-step collection routine. Collections are done in the same order every time. Watch for each step.

  1. Check the job against the order first.
  2. Pre-adjust.
  3. Fit.
  4. Distance, near and in motion.
  5. Care and adaptation script.
  6. First-impression objections.
  7. Book the follow-up.
  8. Check two identifiers.

How to learn from it.

  1. Before the patient comes in, ask what the dispenser will be watching for.
  2. During the dispense, stay quiet and out of the way.
  3. Afterwards, ask one why question.
  4. Write a line in your notebook for each dispense.

Looking ahead to sign-off. Later you will be observed by a registrant. For Level 1 the minimum is 5 observed collections. For Level 2 it is 10 observed single vision dispenses with a mix: a high minus, a high plus, an oblique-axis astigmat, an anisometrope, an NHS voucher, a Transitions or tint, and a second pair. For Level 3 it is 8 observed varifocal and bifocal dispenses, 3 of them measured by you and checked by the signer. In every case the count is a minimum, the signer decides when you are ready, and you answer a 5-question spoken spot-check.

Watch-outs

  • Do not write patient names or details in your notebook.
  • Do not interrupt a dispense. Save the question for afterwards.
  • Do not copy a colleague's habits without asking why. Some are good and some are shortcuts.
  • If unsure, ask your coach.

Tips

  • Shadow a few different colleagues. You will see how styles differ.
  • Bring your notebook to the weekly check-in.

Current practice notes

  • Check with your practice manager: which colleagues you should shadow and when.
  • At each Level the observation count is a minimum plus the signer's "ready" judgement.

Drill

Watch one real dispense this week and write: which step did the dispenser do first at collection, and which was last?

Show model answer

The first step is to check the job against the order. The last is to check two identifiers. (If you shadowed a collection, also name one thing the dispenser did well.)

Today on the floor

Shadow one real dispense with the dispenser's agreement and the patient's agreement (ask before the patient is in the room, and stand back if they say no). Afterwards note which step they did first at collection and which last, and ask them one question.

About 5 minutes, with your coach.

What your coach looks for

  • You stayed in the background and did not speak to the patient
  • Order of steps noted correctly
  • No patient name or detail written down
