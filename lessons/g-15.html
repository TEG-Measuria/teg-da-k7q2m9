The sales rubric: how you will be observed

8 minutes · The conversation

Purpose

You know the ten behaviours observers look for in a dispense, how each is scored, and how to prepare. It matters because you cannot improve what you cannot see, and the rubric tells you exactly what a good dispense looks like.

Worked example

Observer A and Observer B listen to a live dispense. Each scores on their own, using the sheet. The patient is Mrs M, 59, a pharmacist, who wants "just a new pair, the same as before".

The dispenser asks, "What does a normal day look like, and what has worked with your last pair?" (open question: 2). Mrs M mentions long screen days and driving, and the dispenser repeats these back (lifestyle established: 2). She links a screen lens and an anti-reflective coating to those needs (linked: 2), offers good, better and best (three options: 2), and says the price calmly (confident price: 2). When Mrs M says "that is more than I thought", the dispenser says "I get that, what is the main thing?" and answers (objection acknowledged then answered: 2). She asks for the decision (close: 2), but forgets to mention a second pair or sun (0), explains the recall (2), and, when Mrs M asks about a coating she does not know, says "Let me check with the optometrist" (asked when unsure: 2).

Observer A scores 18 of 20. Observer B scores 17. They compare, agree on the discrepancy and write one thing to keep and one thing to change.

The lesson

Ten behaviours, scored 0, 1 or 2

Two observers score each dispense independently, 0, 1 or 2 per behaviour, then compare. The maximum is 20.

  1. Open question: asked at least one open question at the start.
  2. Lifestyle established: found out how the patient uses their eyes (screens, driving, hobbies, work).
  3. Lens linked to need: connected a lens or coating to something the patient said.
  4. Three options: presented good, better and best, with lens before price.
  5. Confident price: said the whole-pair price plainly and then stopped talking.
  6. Objection acknowledged then answered: heard the objection, asked what was behind it, then answered.
  7. Close attempted: asked for the decision once, with the voice falling.
  8. Second pair and sun raised: offered, or recorded as declined.
  9. Recall explained: told the patient when to come back for their next test.
  10. Asked when unsure: checked with a registrant or the record rather than guessing.

What the scores mean

  • 0: not done, or done in a way that worked against the patient.
  • 1: done in part or clumsily, for example a closed question or a rising voice.
  • 2: done clearly, as taught in this course.

How the scoring works

Two observers score on their own, then compare. Where they differ by more than one point on a behaviour, they discuss and agree a score, using the evidence of what was said. Then they write one thing to keep and one thing to change.

How to prepare

  • Practise one behaviour at a time, out loud, with a colleague.
  • Use the objection cards for behaviour 6.
  • Record yourself on the price, and listen for the voice.
  • Pick one behaviour each week and work on it.

What it is not

It is not a script test. You are scored on what the patient experienced, not on repeating words. A dispense where the patient said no but was heard and respected can still score highly.

Watch-outs

  • Do not skip "asked when unsure" because you think it looks weak. It is a strength.
  • Do not read the sheet during a real dispense. Prepare beforehand.
  • Do not treat the second pair as optional. It is scored.
  • Do not mention a discount to raise your score. Offers are a sweetener, never the reason.
  • The rubric covers conversation only. It does not replace the safety rules, such as asking a registrant when unsure.

Tips

  • Say each behaviour in your own words before your first observed dispense.
  • Ask your coach for one example of a 2 and one of a 1 for the same behaviour.
  • Review your scores by behaviour, not just the total.

Current practice notes

  • The Dispensing Conversation observation sheet uses a similar approach (1 if seen, 0 if not); this course rubric scores 0, 1 or 2.
  • Check with your manager the pass mark and who counts as an observer in your practice. Check with your practice manager.

Drill

Score yourself 0, 1 or 2 on each of the ten behaviours after your next practice dispense with a colleague. Write one thing to keep and one to change.

Show model answer

A completed list of ten scores, such as 2, 1, 2, 2, 1, 2, 2, 0, 1, 2 (total 15), plus one thing to keep (for example "I stopped after the price") and one to change (for example "I forgot the second pair").

Today on the floor

Role-play a short dispense with a colleague. A coach and one other person each score you privately on the ten behaviours (0, 1 or 2), then compare. Pick the two lowest scores to practise next. This is practice, not a pass or fail.

About 5 minutes, with your coach.

What your coach looks for

  • Open question and lifestyle in the first minute
  • Three options, then a confident price
  • Close attempted and second pair raised once
  • Asks when unsure
