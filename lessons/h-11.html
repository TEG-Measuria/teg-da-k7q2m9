The 90-day check: what amazing looks like

7 minutes · Varifocal specialist

Purpose

You can explain what excellent dispensing looks like at Level 3, and how the 90-day check measures it as a trend, not as a pass or fail.

Worked example

Dispenser J has been at Level 3 for ninety days. Her coach sits down with her to look at four things. First, her remake rate: three remakes from forty varifocal jobs, lower than last quarter. Second, her second-pair rate: more patients bought a second pair. Third, the observed rubric: the coach watched two collections and scored them against the eight steps. Fourth, patient feedback: several comments praised her explanations. The coach says, "This is a good trend. The remake rate has dropped; let us look at the two remakes together and see what we can learn." There is no pass or fail; there is a conversation about what to keep and what to work on.

The lesson

"Amazing" at TEG is Level 3 plus a 90-day check. Level 3 means you have completed the training and the observed assessments. The 90-day check looks at how you are doing in real life.

The four measures

  1. Remake rate. How many of your jobs were remade, and why. A remake is not shameful; patterns tell you what to learn.
  2. Second-pair rate. How often patients choose a second pair after you have shown the options. It reflects trust and good conversations.
  3. The observed rubric. A coach watches real collections or sales conversations and scores them against the standard.
  4. Patient feedback. Comments, reviews and calls.

A trend, never pass or fail. These are not targets to hit or exams to pass. They are a trend over time, discussed with your coach. A single bad week proves nothing; a steady pattern shows where to focus. The point is learning, not blame.

What the conversation covers

  • What is going well and should be repeated.
  • Which remakes are avoidable and what would prevent them.
  • Which conversations went well and why.
  • What support or practice you need.

What it does not do. It does not rank staff, decide pay or remove authority on its own. Any decision about your level is made by your practice manager and the owner, using more than these figures.

Keeping the data fair. Remakes caused by a prescription change, a supplier fault or patient damage are noted as such, so you are not marked down for what you did not cause.

Your role. Keep your own notes; bring questions; accept feedback as information.

Watch-outs

  • Do not treat a figure as a target to be hit at any cost.
  • Do not hide a remake; recording it helps everyone.
  • Do not compare yourself with colleagues; compare with your own trend.
  • Do not skip the observed rubric.

Tips

  • Ask your coach what "good" looks like before the check.
  • Bring one example of a conversation you are proud of.
  • Look at remakes as learning.

Current practice notes

  • Level 3 plus a 90-day check is the standard for an excellent dispenser.
  • Track returns and remakes in the tracker and record the reason.
  • Check with your manager how the 90-day check is scheduled and who runs it in your practice. Check with your practice manager.

Drill

Name the four measures of the 90-day check and say why a single bad week proves nothing.

Show model answer

Remake rate, second-pair rate, observed rubric, patient feedback. A single week is too small to show a pattern, and these are a trend discussed with a coach, never pass or fail.

Today on the floor

With your coach, talk through the four measures of the 90-day check (remake rate, second-pair rate, observed rubric, patient feedback) for your own recent work. Choose one thing to try this week. This is a trend chat, not a pass or fail.

About 4 minutes, with your coach.

What your coach looks for

  • Names the four measures
  • Treats numbers as a trend, not a target
  • Chooses one specific thing to practise
  • Says what support or practice would help
