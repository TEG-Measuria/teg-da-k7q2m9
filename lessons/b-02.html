Single vision lenses: stock and surfaced

7 minutes · Lenses

Purpose

After this lesson you can explain the difference between a stock and a surfaced single vision lens, say why a patient might be offered one or the other, and talk about single vision pricing without typing a figure from memory.

Worked example

Ms R, 29, a nurse, has a mild prescription and wants glasses for the evening and for driving. She asks, "Why is a basic pair so much cheaper than the one you showed me with the Eyezen name?"

You explain it in three steps. First: a basic single vision lens is a good lens, hard-coated and multi-coated, with a clear view. Second: for her mild prescription, an off-the-shelf stock lens or a simple lab-made lens gives her everything she needs for the road and the evening. Third: the premium range adds special designs for particular jobs, such as heavy screen work, and she has not mentioned that need. You ask her one more question, "Do you spend a lot of the day on a screen?" She says not really. You offer the single vision lens, show her the price on the price list, and tell her she can add a clear anti-reflection coating or a sun pair later if her life changes.

She leaves having understood why the price differs, and she has not been sold something she does not need.

The lesson

A stock lens is made in advance by the manufacturer in a range of powers and then cut to shape for the frame. It is quick and sensible for lower and common prescriptions. Because it comes in set steps, it cannot be shaped to every prescription, so the strongest and most unusual prescriptions will not be available in stock.

A surfaced lens (also called a lab-made, made-to-order or digitally surfaced lens) starts as a thicker blank. The lab grinds and polishes the back surface to the exact prescription, and today this is usually done by computer-controlled equipment. This is how stronger prescriptions, higher index materials, astigmatism and most coatings and tints are catered for.

What the patient sees is not "stock" or "surfaced". They see a clear lens or a thinner one, a lens for the road or for the screen, and a price. Your job is to translate. A short way to say it: "Your prescription is made specially for you at the lab, so it is exactly right for your eyes."

Single vision on our list is a hard multi-coated lens (HMC). HMC means a scratch-resistant hard coating and an anti-reflection multi-coat. It is sold in the four index bands (see b-07) and in clear or Transitions. For the current price, check Price-it or the group price list. For example, you can show a patient the single vision clear row with £120 for a pair at the standard index, and the Gold member price is £96 for the same pair.

When single vision is the right answer: for a patient who needs one distance, has no special screen or driving demands, or wants a sensible first pair at a lower price. It is also the right answer for many children (the registrant leads for under-16s) and for patients who just want a spare pair.

When to look at a premium single vision design instead: heavy screen use or tired-eyes complaints (see b-03), or a driving focus (see b-05).

Watch-outs

  • Do not describe a basic single vision lens as "cheap" or "poor". It is a good lens that fits many needs.
  • Do not promise a lens will be ready by a date until you know whether it is a stock or a surfaced lens. Surfaced lenses depend on the lab.
  • Never override a price in Optix One (desktop); if a price looks wrong, ask.
  • If a prescription is strong or has a high cylinder, ask a registrant or an experienced colleague about the best index and design before quoting.

Tips

  • Start with the cheapest sensible lens that meets the need, then show the one step up and say what it adds.
  • Use the patient's words: "so it works for the road and the evening".
  • Write the reason for the lens choice on the dispense.

Current practice notes

  • Single vision is priced per pair by index band and clear or Transitions on the group price list.
  • Which lens types are held in stock at your practice and the usual lab turnaround: Check with your practice manager: what is held in stock and how long a surfaced pair takes.

Drill

Out loud, in 20 seconds, explain to a patient (a "Mr T, 34, driver, mild prescription") why his single vision pair costs less than an Eyezen pair, without using the words "stock", "surfaced" or any jargon.

Show model answer

"These are good lenses with a hard, scratch-resistant coat and an anti-reflection coat, made to your prescription. The Eyezen lenses have an extra design for people who spend hours on a screen. If that is not you, these will do the job well. If your day changes, we can look at the other ones."

Today on the floor

Explain to a colleague, in 20 seconds, why a single vision pair costs less than a screen-use pair. Avoid the words stock and surfaced.

About 3 minutes, with a colleague.

What your coach looks for

  • Plain words, no jargon
  • Honest about what the extra buys
  • Under 30 seconds
