How our lens price list works

7 minutes · Pricing

Purpose

After this lesson you can find the right cell on the group lens price list and read out the price per pair without guessing. Patients trust a price that is given calmly and correctly the first time.

Worked example

Mr B, 52, accountant, wants Eyezen Start clear lenses. His prescription is about -3.00 in each eye.

  1. Find the row: Eyezen Start.
  2. Find the column: the printed list shows "2.25 to 3.00 DS" as a guide. That sits under "Thin & light", the 1.6 index (Ormix).
  3. Choose clear, not Transitions.
  4. Read across: the cell says £280 for the pair.
  5. Say it as a pair price: "That is the price for both lenses."

You did not work out anything. You read one cell. If you cannot find a cell, or the cell says "not available", you stop and ask rather than make up a price.

The lesson

One list for the whole group. All four practices use the same lens price list. Staff cannot override a price in Optix One (desktop), so the list and the till always agree.

Every price is per pair. A cell shows the price of both lenses together. Never quote a single lens unless a colleague asks you to.

The list has three directions.

  • Down the side are the lens designs: Eyezen Start, Eyezen Boost, Road Pilot, E-reader (Varilux Immersia MID), Comfort Max, XR (XR Design or XR Pro, same price), single vision (SV), D28 bifocal, special-order bifocal, Elite (Visio Digital varifocal) and Tailor Made (Nova Plus 3.0 varifocal).
  • Across the top are four index bands: Standard 1.5 (Orma), Thin & light 1.6 (Ormix), Thinner & lighter 1.67 (Stylis) and Thinnest & lightest 1.74 (Lineis).
  • Each band has a clear price and a Transitions price.

The power labels are a guide. The printed list labels the columns "Up to 2.00 DS", "2.25 to 3.00", "3.25 to 5.00" and "Over 5.00 DS". These are a guide to which index usually suits a power. The index for a particular patient also depends on the frame, the cylinder and what the patient wants the lenses to look like. If the choice is not obvious, ask a dispensing optician or optometrist.

Some cells are blank or say "not available". For example, there is no 1.74 Road Pilot. If the cell is empty, that combination is not sold. Offer the nearest index that is.

Two sets of numbers. There is the standard price and the Gold member price. The Gold column is for patients on the Eyecare Plan Gold. You will learn that column in the next lessons. You never mix them: a Gold member is quoted from the Gold column, everyone else from the standard one.

Use the price-it tool to practise. Open Price-it and practise reading cells until it is automatic.

Never type a price from memory. Prices change. The list dated November 2026 is the source. If a price on screen in Optix One (desktop) looks different from the list, do not change it. Tell your practice manager.

Watch-outs

  • Quoting one lens price when the list shows the price per pair.
  • Using the printed power column as a rule rather than a guide.
  • Reading the Transitions cell when the patient asked for clear (they sit side by side).
  • Quoting a cell that says "not available".
  • Overriding a price in Optix One (desktop) because it "looks wrong". Staff cannot do this and it breaks the VAT split. Ask instead.

Tips

  • Put a finger on the row, then slide to the column, then pick clear or Transitions. Say the three things out loud.
  • Say "per pair" every time you give a price.
  • If the patient is between two bands, quote both so they can choose.

Current practice notes

  • One group price list, priced per pair, by design, index band and clear or Transitions (list dated November 2026).
  • Staff cannot override a price in the till; only Administrators can (from 6 Nov 2026).
  • Index names: Orma 1.5, Ormix 1.6, Stylis 1.67, Lineis 1.74.

Drill

Without looking at the list, write down the three choices you must make to find a price on the lens list (the three directions). Then open Price-it and find the price for Eyezen Boost, Thinner & lighter 1.67, Transitions. Say it out loud as a pair price.

Show model answer

The three choices are: the lens design (row), the index band (column), and clear or Transitions. For the example, you read the Eyezen Boost row, the 1.67 band, the Transitions cell, and say "That is the price for the pair." (use the figure from the tool).

Today on the floor

Open the printed lens price list. Find the price for a design, an index and a coating by going across the three directions. Read it aloud.

About 3 minutes, with your coach.

What your coach looks for

  • Price read from the list, not memory
  • All three directions used
  • Says price per pair
