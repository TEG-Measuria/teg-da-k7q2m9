Price objections

8 minutes · The conversation

Purpose

You can answer the five common price objections calmly, using the five-step method, without cutting a price or criticising anyone. It matters because most price objections are about value, not money, and a clear answer often keeps the sale.

Worked example

Mr D, 49, electrician, hears the price of the pair and says, "It's cheaper online."

The dispenser acknowledges: "Fair enough, and I get why you would look." She clarifies: "What would you be comparing it with?" He says a pair he saw advertised for a low price. She answers, without criticism: "I cannot speak for theirs, but let me show you exactly what is in ours and why the optometrist advises it for you." She goes through the lens design that suits his work, the coating for the glare off his screen, and the fact that his lenses will be measured and centred for him by a person who sees him. She checks: "Does that make sense?" And closes: "Shall we go through the options?"

Mr D picks the middle option. If he had not, she would have said, "No problem at all, I will note what the optometrist advised and hold your quote."

The lesson

Why people say it

"It is too expensive" often means "I do not yet see why it is worth it". They cannot know it is expensive until you have said the price, and until you have shown what it gives them.

Five common price objections

  1. Too expensive. Agree, then break the thought with a short true story and set the scale. Use real figures from the price list, never from memory. Then go through the gold standard and say the price of each option before anything is ordered.
  2. Cheaper elsewhere. Do not criticise the other shop. Compare what is included: the design, the coatings, the measuring, the after-care, the warranty wording to be confirmed with the practice manager.
  3. Online. Same method. Explain that your lenses are measured and centred for the individual wearer, and that you are there if anything feels wrong. Do not say that buying online is illegal or unsafe.
  4. Why does a thinner lens cost so much more? A thinner lens uses a more advanced material and has a thinner edge, so the glasses look neater. Do not promise it is lighter: thin does not always mean light (b-07, b-08), so only say that if you have checked the product information. Show it by laying thick and thin samples side by side. Ask whether looks matter to them. If not, say a thicker lens is perfectly fine.
  5. I will come back on payday. Of course. Write down what the optometrist advised, hold the quote, and agree a date you will follow up. Do not pressure. Do follow up.

Tools that help

  • Name the worry: "You are probably thinking this sounds expensive."
  • Set the scale: show the price of the best option first, so the others feel reasonable. Use real list prices, via Price-it.
  • Compromise: "Let us look at where we could compromise."
  • A true story, with no names: "I had a patient who was worried about cost..." If you have none, say "A lot of patients tell me..." and never invent.

What you do not do

Do not cut the price. Staff cannot override a price; if a price looks wrong, ask. Do not make up a discount. Do not tell them you can "see what you can do" unless it is a real, approved code.

Watch-outs

  • Never criticise another retailer or an online seller.
  • Do not state anything unsafe or illegal about online glasses.
  • Do not invent a story or a saving.
  • Do not skip acknowledging: the first words matter most.
  • If a price looks wrong, ask a manager.

Tips

  • Say the price of the whole pair, then stop talking.
  • Carry two thick-and-thin lens samples.
  • At "payday", agree the follow-up date in the diary before they leave.

Current practice notes

  • Prices come from the group price list, per pair, by design and index band. Use Price-it to look them up.
  • Staff cannot override a price in Optix One (desktop); only Administrators can.
  • The Dispensing Conversation module "That sounds expensive" shows the story-and-scale method.
  • Check with your manager how long a quote is held and the warranty wording. Check with your practice manager.

Drill

With a colleague. They say, "Why does a thinner lens cost so much more?" Answer in three short sentences.

Show model answer

"That is a fair question. It is a more advanced material, so the lens is thinner at the edge and the glasses look neater. I would not promise it is lighter, as thin does not always mean light. Does the look matter to you? If not, the standard one is perfectly good."

Today on the floor

A colleague plays a patient with a price objection, using the price objection cards. Role-play it with the five steps, then swap. You may not cut a price or criticise anyone; staff cannot change a price.

About 5 minutes, with a colleague.

What your coach looks for

  • Finds which part worries them
  • Ties the answer to what they said earlier
  • Does not discount or criticise another provider
  • Asks once whether to go ahead or look at other options
