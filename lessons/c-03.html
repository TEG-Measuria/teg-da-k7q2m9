Fit first: bridge, sides and ears

7 minutes · Frames, styling and measuring

Purpose

You can judge whether a frame fits a patient's nose, face and ears before you do anything else. A frame that fits well stays put, looks right and lets the lenses work as intended. A frame that does not fit cannot be saved by the lenses.

Worked example

Miss R, 27, a graphic designer, tries a frame she loves. Standing in front of the mirror you check four things in order.

  1. Bridge: the frame rests on her nose evenly on both sides and does not pinch or leave a mark. It does not slide down when she looks down. Good.
  2. Width: the frame front is the same width as her face at the temples, not wider and not narrower. Good.
  3. Sides: they run straight back and touch the side of her head lightly. At the ears the sides bend down and follow the line of her ear. The tips are on the ear, not digging into the skin. Good.
  4. Position: her eyes sit in the middle of the lenses, and the top of the frame follows the line of her eyebrows. Her eyelashes do not touch the lens.

Everything is right except the bridge: on the left it sits a little higher than on the right, so the frame tilts. You note it for a small adjustment (c-04) and do not call the fit done until it is level. You tell her: "That is a good shape on you. I'll make a small adjustment to level it, then we will check it again."

The lesson

Order of checks: look at the frame on the face, not in your hand

  1. Frame front width against the face. The outer edge of the frame should line up with the widest part of the face, or sit a little inside.
  2. Bridge. Whether it is a plastic saddle bridge or a metal bridge with pads, it should spread the weight over a wide enough area to be comfortable. On the nose, look at the contact area from the front and from the side.
  3. Eyes in the lens. For most prescriptions, the pupils should be roughly in the middle of the lens opening, both horizontally and vertically.
  4. Eyebrows and cheeks. The top of the frame should follow or sit just under the brow line. The bottom of the frame should not rest on the cheeks.
  5. Sides and ears. Sides should be long enough to bend behind the ear and not so long that they slide off. They should not press hard on the side of the head.
  6. Balance. The frame should sit level. Tilt it by eye from the front and the side.

A fit that has "settled": wearers move their heads, smile, look down. Ask the patient to look down, shake their head gently and smile. A good fit stays where it is.

Why fit comes first

  • Slipping glasses change the optical centre position, so the patient looks through the wrong part of the lens.
  • Frames that pinch cause headaches, red marks and a patient who stops wearing them.
  • A frame that does not suit the face looks wrong however good the lenses are. Patients judge your work by how it looks and feels.

Fit versus style Choose the frame for fit first and style second. Be honest about fit: "This one won't sit well on your nose, so I wouldn't choose it. This one fits much better." That honesty is how you build trust.

Watch-outs

  • A frame can look good but fit badly. Do not sell on looks alone.
  • Do not make the final fit judgement with the frame in your hand. Look at it on the patient.
  • Wide faces in narrow frames, and narrow faces in wide frames, are the most common problems.
  • Do not bend or heat a frame to force a fit it cannot reach. If it will not fit, choose another.
  • If unsure, ask a colleague to check.

Tips

  • Look at the patient from the front, from the side and from slightly above.
  • Ask: "Does anything press or feel tight?"
  • Fit the frame before you do any measuring. Measuring a poorly fitted frame gives wrong figures (c-10).

Current practice notes

  • Check with your practice manager: who checks the final fit at collection in your practice, and how a fit check is recorded.
  • Fit at order and fit at collection are two separate checks.

Drill

A patient tries a frame that is slightly wider than her face and slips when she looks down. Say out loud what you tell her and what you do next.

Show model answer

"This one is a bit wide for you, so it will keep slipping. I would rather find one that fits properly." Do not try to force a fit with adjustments. Choose a narrower frame or a different bridge, then repeat the checks: width, bridge, eyes in the lens, brow and cheeks, sides and ears, and balance.

Today on the floor

Fit a frame on a colleague. Check the nose, the sides and the ears, and say one thing you would change.

About 4 minutes, with your coach.

What your coach looks for

  • Checks bridge, sides and ears in order
  • Frame sits level and does not slip
  • Says what to change without forcing the frame
