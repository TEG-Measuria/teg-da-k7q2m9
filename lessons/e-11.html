Out-of-stock frames, lead times and chasing

7 minutes · Systems

Purpose

After this lesson you can keep orders moving: spot jobs waiting on a frame, chase suppliers and tell patients before they ask. Patients value updates far more than discounts.

Worked example

Mr Q, 67, retired driver, ordered glasses with a frame the supplier has run out of. His promise date is in nine days.

  1. In Optix One (desktop) open Patient Orders and find his order. Check it has an order number.
  2. See the frame status: the frame is on order. The lens order stays pending until it is processed.
  3. Add a note that the frame is ordered. Put a yellow tag on his tray.
  4. Chase the frame supplier and record the date they expect it.
  5. Phone Mr Q now with the new estimated time, ask how often he wants updates, and record the call in the Patient Order Notes.
  6. Re-check status when the order is due and update him again.

The lesson

Daily checks.

  1. In Optix One (desktop) open Patient Orders and check every order under To be processed has an order number.
  2. Check each order shows the frame status: posted, tele-shaped or still awaiting a frame. An order awaiting a frame stays pending until the lens order is processed. Add a note that the frame is ordered.
  3. Put a yellow tag on the tray of any job with a frame on order. Move ordered frames to In Progress so they can be tracked.
  4. Open Work in Progress and filter by promise date, soonest first.
  5. Watch overdue orders. Start chasing a week before the due date, so problems such as broken frames or unplaced orders can be fixed while keeping the patient informed.
  6. Check each order's status with the supplier portal, for example Essilor. Chase manual orders by phone and note each supplier reference.
  7. Put a red tag on any tray past its promise date.

Telling the patient.

  • Update patients before they contact you.
  • Give the new estimated arrival time once you know it.
  • Say the order is being monitored and ask how and how often they want updates.
  • Record the estimate and every contact, including the method, in the Patient Order Notes.
  • If you leave a voicemail, follow up within a few days by another method and record every attempt.

Lead times. Typical lead times differ by supplier and product. Check with your practice manager: the usual lead times for your suppliers.

Do not promise compensation. Patients value updates more than discounts. Never offer a discount to make up for a delay unless your practice manager agrees.

Reasons for delay. Find out why: out-of-stock frame, manufacturer issue, production problem.

These are screen steps from 2024 documents. Check with your practice manager: the order statuses and screen names in your version of Optix One (desktop).

Watch-outs

  • Waiting for the patient to ask.
  • Leaving a frame on order without a note or tag.
  • Promising a date you cannot be sure of.
  • Offering compensation.

Tips

  • Set reminders through the day to check overdue jobs.
  • Use a standard wording for delay messages.
  • Use the patient's name and Optix ID when emailing a supplier.

Current practice notes

  • Chase from a week before the due date; yellow tag for frame on order; red tag past the promise date.
  • Staff use the client's name and Optix ID in emails to suppliers.
  • Steps come from 2024 documents and are not yet checked on the live screen.
  • Check with your practice manager: typical lead times per supplier, and the screen names in Optix One (desktop).

Drill

A frame will be two weeks late. List the first four things you do today.

Show model answer

add a note and yellow tag, chase the supplier for a date, phone the patient with the new estimate and ask how they want updates, record the contact in the Patient Order Notes.

Today on the floor

With your manager or a colleague, open Patient Orders and Work in Progress on the practice system on a training or test record only, never a named patient's live record, and without copying any patient details down. Find the list sorted by promise date and point to an order waiting on a frame. Say what you would tell the patient, when, and what you would not offer.

About 4 minutes, with your coach.

What your coach looks for

  • Finds Work in Progress and sorts by promise date
  • Explains the yellow and red tag meaning at your practice
  • Says patients are updated before they ask
  • Says not to offer compensation unless the manager agrees
