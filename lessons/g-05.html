Saying the price with confidence

6 minutes · The conversation

Purpose

You can say the price of a pair of glasses plainly and calmly, then stop talking. It matters because patients take their cue from you: if you sound unsure about the price, they will feel unsure about it too.

Worked example

Mrs H, 66, retired nurse, has chosen a frame and the middle lens option. The dispenser has the total on screen.

Version one: "So, er, with the frame and the lenses it comes to, um, I'm afraid it's quite a lot, it's just..." and then a figure said upwards, followed by "but we do have offers." Mrs H hears a worry, and says she will think about it.

Version two: "With the frame and both lenses, the whole pair comes to" (the figure) "." The dispenser lets her voice fall, looks at Mrs H calmly and says nothing else. Four seconds go by. Mrs H says, "That is what I expected. Can I collect it next week?"

The words in version two were shorter. The confidence was in the delivery, the single sentence and the silence.

The lesson

Say the whole pair

Say the price of the whole pair, frame and lenses together, in one sentence. Patients buy glasses, not parts. If you give the frame price and the lens price separately, they add them up and feel each one. The total should be the figure you read from the quote, which comes from the group price list through Optix One (desktop).

Say it once, plainly

Say the number once, plainly, with your voice falling at the end, and then stop talking. A rising voice makes the price a question. A fall makes it a fact.

Do not apologise

Do not apologise, do not say just, and do not wince. Phrases like "I'm afraid", "it's only" or "it's a bit" tell the patient how to feel. The price is the price of a well-made product that was explained to them.

Silence is thinking

Silence after the price is the patient thinking, not a problem for you to fix. Count to five in your head. Most people who are going to buy say something in that time. If you speak first, you are probably talking yourself out of a sale or offering a discount nobody asked for.

Name the worry before the price if you sense it

If the patient looks tense, say their worry out loud first: "You may be thinking this sounds expensive." Then give the price. It takes the pressure off.

Price before order

Give the price before anything is ordered. A patient who is surprised at the till at collection will not come back.

Never override

Never override a price in the system. If a price looks wrong, ask. Staff cannot override prices, and an override breaks the VAT split.

Look it up, do not guess

Every price comes from the list. Practise with Price-it. Do not say a figure from memory, and do not quote a price you have not checked.

Watch-outs

  • Do not lead with a discount to soften the price. Offers are a sweetener, never the reason.
  • Do not say the price while looking at the screen. Look at the patient.
  • Do not add "but" after the price.
  • Do not fill the silence. Count to five.
  • If the patient asks for a price you cannot find, say you will check and ask. If unsure, ask.

Tips

  • Practise saying a made-up figure out loud ten times until it sounds like the weather.
  • Record yourself and listen for the rising voice.
  • Pair the price with the benefit just before it: "This is the one that gives you the clear driving vision, and the whole pair is..."

Current practice notes

  • All prices come from the group price list, per pair, by design and index band. Use Price-it to practise.
  • Staff cannot override a price in Optix One (desktop); only Administrators can (from 6 November 2026). If a price looks wrong, ask.
  • The Dispensing Conversation: price is always given before anything is ordered.

Drill

With a colleague: say a made-up whole-pair figure three times. Once with a rising voice and an apology, once flat and quick, once calm with a falling voice followed by five seconds of silence. Ask your colleague which one they would trust.

Show model answer

The third. One sentence, the whole pair, a voice that falls at the end, then five seconds of silence while the patient thinks.

Today on the floor

Say a whole-pair price out loud to a colleague, taken from price-it or the price list (never made up), then stop talking. Do it three times. The colleague listens for apology, hesitation or filling the silence.

About 3 minutes, with a colleague.

What your coach looks for

  • Plain, calm voice
  • Says the whole-pair price, not just the parts
  • Stops talking after the price
  • No apology or hedging words
