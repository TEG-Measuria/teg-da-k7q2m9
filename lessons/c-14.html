Cheek, lash and slipping problems

7 minutes · Frames, styling and measuring

Purpose

You can find the cause of three common complaints (frames resting on the cheeks, lashes touching the lens, and glasses that slip) and choose a frame or an adjustment that solves it, or know when to ask for help.

Worked example

Mrs M, 72, retired, returns two weeks after collecting new glasses. She says: "They keep sliding down my nose, and they leave a mark on my cheeks." You look at her wearing them before touching anything.

  1. You see the frame resting on her cheeks when she smiles. The lens is too deep for her face.
  2. The nose pads are too close together, so the frame sits too low.
  3. The sides are slightly loose at the ear.

You do not blame her or the pads. You find the three causes and fix them in order. A small pad adjustment (on a metal frame) lifts the frame. A small bend to the sides fits them to the ear. The cheek problem stays, because the lens is deep. You say honestly: "The adjustments will help the slipping, but the frame is a bit deep for your cheek line. If it still marks, I can show you a shallower frame." Two days later she comes back, still marked. You show her a shallower frame, and she is happy. You did not argue; you solved it.

The lesson

Frames resting on the cheeks

  • Causes: lens or frame too deep, bridge sitting too low, or the pantoscopic tilt too small so the bottom of the frame swings forward onto the cheek.
  • Fixes: raise the frame with the pads (metal frames), a higher-bridge frame, a shallower frame, or small tilt adjustments by a trained person (c-04, c-12).
  • For prevention, check when the patient smiles: the cheeks rise.

Lashes touching the lens

  • Causes: the lens too close to the eye (short vertex distance), a frame that is very close fitting, or long lashes.
  • Fixes: push the frame a little further from the eye with pads (metal frames), or choose a frame with a deeper bridge or longer pads. For plastic, a frame with a built-up bridge. A frame with a larger eye shape can also help.
  • Check by asking the patient to blink, look up and look down.

Slipping

  • Causes: loose sides, the bridge too wide, pads too close or too small, sides too long or curved wrongly, a heavy lens, or the frame not tight enough at the ear.
  • Fixes: adjust the sides and pads, change to a narrower bridge, silicone pads or a different frame. Weight may be reduced by choosing a thinner lens or a smaller frame (c-08).

Order

  1. Look at the patient wearing the frame. Do not guess.
  2. Ask what they notice and when: when they look down? when they smile? when they run?
  3. Find the cause.
  4. Choose the smallest fix: pad, side, then frame.
  5. Re-check the fit, and ask them to come back if there is a problem.
  6. Know your limit: ask a qualified colleague if you are not trained to do the adjustment or if the frame is old or fragile (c-04).

Tell the patient what to expect "This will take a few days to settle." If the problem continues, say "we will find another frame."

Where the issue is in the lens, not the frame If the patient says things look blurred or off as well as the frame problem, do not treat it as a fit complaint only. Tell a registrant.

Watch-outs

  • Do not adjust a frame you could not repair if it broke without asking (c-04).
  • Do not tell the patient it is "their face".
  • Do not ignore marks or sore spots; they are a sign of a poor fit.
  • A frame that slips and then changes the position of the lenses affects how they see. If the patient also reports eyestrain or blurred vision, tell a registrant.
  • If unsure, ask a registrant.

Tips

  • Smile in the mirror: it shows if the cheeks touch.
  • Choose silicone pads for slippery noses.
  • Say sorry for the problem and show you will fix it.

Current practice notes

  • A returning complaint after collection is handled as an aftercare fit check first. Check with your practice manager: who handles remakes and frame swaps for fit complaints at your practice.

Drill

A patient says her lashes keep brushing her new lenses. Say out loud what you check, what you try first and what you do if that fails.

Show model answer

Look at her wearing the frame and ask her to blink, look up and look down. The lens is probably too close to the eye. On a metal frame, adjust the pads to move the frame a little further out. If that does not solve it, show a frame with a built-up bridge or a larger eye shape. If I am not trained to adjust it, or the frame is fragile, ask a qualified colleague.

Today on the floor

With a spare frame, show a colleague what you check for lashes touching the lens, cheek contact and slipping, and what you try first.

About 4 minutes, with your coach.

What your coach looks for

  • Checks fit before changing anything
  • Tries a simple adjustment first
  • Passes to the coach when it will not fix
