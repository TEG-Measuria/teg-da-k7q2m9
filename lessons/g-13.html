Closing and booking the collection

6 minutes · The conversation

Purpose

You can close a dispense by asking for the decision once, in a voice that falls, and then book the collection so the patient leaves with a date. It matters because a sale that ends without a next step often drifts, and a booked collection brings the patient back.

Worked example

Mr S, 45, bus driver, has chosen a frame and a lens. The dispenser has said the price of the whole pair and waited. Mr S says, "That sounds fine."

The dispenser says: "Are you happy with that? Great." Her voice falls and she carries on: "These will take about a week. Would Thursday or Friday suit you for collection?" Mr S says Friday after work. She writes it in the diary, repeats the day and time, checks his phone number, and says: "We will text you when they are ready. When you collect, we will check the fit and make sure you are comfortable, and I will explain how often to come back for a check." She walks him to the door.

There was no pause, no "would you like to go ahead?", and no repeated question.

The lesson

The walk-on close

Ask for the decision once, and carry on as if it is agreed. This is sometimes called a walk-on close. Say it flat and friendly, like a waiter asking, "Shall I show you to your table?" The voice falls at the end, and you keep moving.

  • Do this: "Are you alright with that? Great, come this way."
  • Not this: "Would you like to go ahead...?" and a pause. It sounds optional.

Or the authority-and-choice close

"Are you happy to keep with what the optometrist has advised, or would you prefer to do something different?" It keeps the optometrist's authority and lets the patient decide.

Stop if they hesitate

If the patient hesitates, goes quiet or asks a question, stop the walk-on and answer it. Then close again once.

Book the collection before they leave

After the yes, give them a next step straight away.

  1. Say how long it will take, using the lead time your practice gives you.
  2. Offer two choices of day: "Thursday or Friday?" Two choices are easier than an open question.
  3. Write it in the diary and repeat it back.
  4. Check their contact details and how they would like to be told.
  5. Say what happens at collection: a fit check, how to wear the glasses, and what to do if something feels odd.
  6. Mention the recall: when they should come back for their next eye test.

Write it down

Make sure the dispense record shows the options chosen and any declined extras, such as a second pair, so the next visit can pick up.

Price before order

The patient must have heard the price before anything is ordered.

Watch-outs

  • Do not ask for the decision twice.
  • Do not close before the price has been given.
  • Do not leave without a collection date or a clear reason why not.
  • Do not promise a date you cannot meet. If unsure of the lead time, ask.
  • Do not take or process payment outside the practice process. Check with your practice manager.

Tips

  • Practise the closing phrase out loud until your voice falls naturally.
  • Offer two days, not "when would suit you?".
  • Say the recall line every time: "We will see you for your next eye test in about a year, or sooner if anything changes."

Current practice notes

  • The close in the Dispensing Conversation: "Are you alright with that? Great, come this way."
  • Check with your manager typical lead time for collection and how deposits and payment are taken. Check with your practice manager.
  • Check with your manager how collections and recall are recorded in Optix One (desktop) in your practice. Check with your practice manager.

Drill

Out loud: the patient has agreed to the pair. Say your closing and booking lines, in four short sentences.

Show model answer

"Great, that is all agreed. They will take about a week, so would Thursday or Friday suit you for collection? I will put it in the diary and text you when they are ready. When you collect we will check the fit, and I will tell you when to come back for your next eye test."

Today on the floor

Role-play closing a dispense with a colleague: ask for the decision once with a falling voice, then book the collection and give a date. Swap roles. Practice only.

About 4 minutes, with a colleague.

What your coach looks for

  • Asks for the decision once
  • Voice falls and then stops talking
  • Books a date before they leave and says when it will be ready
  • Accepts a not yet kindly
