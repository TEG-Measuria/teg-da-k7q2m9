Eyecare plans: Pay as you go, Essential, Gold, Contacts

7 minutes · Pricing

Purpose

After this lesson you can describe the four Eyecare Plan options in plain words and help a patient see which one fits. A good plan conversation saves the patient money and keeps them with the practice.

Worked example

Mr C, 38, software developer, wears daily contact lenses and has an eye test due. He asks, "Is the plan worth it?"

  1. Ask what he buys: contact lenses and an eye test each year.
  2. Look at the four plans. The Contacts plan costs 11 pounds a month and covers the eye exam and contact lens check-ups and gives 50% off contact lenses.
  3. Compare with Pay as you go, where each item is paid for at its normal price.
  4. Tell him what each option includes and let him choose.

You do not tell him which to pick. You show what each does for someone who buys what he buys.

The lesson

There are four options.

Plan Monthly cost In a nutshell
Pay as you go None Normal price for each service
Essential 8 pounds Eye examinations and scans fully covered
Gold 14 pounds Everything in Essential plus spectacle discounts and damage cover
Contacts 11 pounds Eye care plus contact lens fitting and check-ups, with contact lens discounts

What Essential and Gold fully cover. The comprehensive eye examination, Optomap retinal scan, 3D glaucoma and macular scans, emergency examinations, glaucoma eye pressure phasing and dry eye assessments.

What Gold adds. 30% off frames, 20% off prescription lenses, 50% off additional spectacles bought within 90 days of the first pair, 50% off contact lenses, 20% off care products, and 2-year accidental damage spectacle cover.

Family cover. Family cover is available as an extra monthly amount on Essential and Gold.

How to talk about it.

  • Ask what they buy in a year (glasses, contacts, tests).
  • Put the options side by side without pressure.
  • Say what each plan includes, and what it does not.
  • Never say a plan will "save money" unless you have worked it out for their purchases.

No medical claims. The plan covers services. It does not promise eye health outcomes.

Do not sign anyone up without their say. Joining happens in a conversation, with the patient agreeing to the monthly payment. If the patient has questions about how payments work, ask your practice manager.

Watch-outs

  • Saying "Gold is best for everyone". For some patients Essential or Pay as you go is right.
  • Forgetting Gold is also the plan that unlocks the Gold price columns.
  • Promising cover you are not sure of. Ask.
  • Signing up a patient who has not agreed to the monthly payment.

Tips

  • Use the plan sheet in the practice rather than reciting from memory.
  • Ask about the whole household before mentioning family cover.
  • A patient buying new glasses today is the best moment to compare Gold with Pay as you go.

Current practice notes

  • Plans: Pay as you go, Essential (8 pounds a month), Gold (14 pounds a month), Contacts (11 pounds a month).
  • Essential and Gold fully cover the comprehensive eye examination and scans; Gold adds frame, lens and care discounts and 2-year accidental damage cover.
  • Check with your practice manager: how a patient is signed up and how payments are taken.

Drill

A patient wears glasses, has an eye test due and buys one new pair every two years. Write two sentences comparing Pay as you go with Gold for her, without saying which she should pick.

Show model answer

"On Pay as you go you pay normal prices for the test and the glasses. On Gold you pay a monthly fee, the test and scans are covered, and you get 30% off frames and 20% off lenses." You then ask which suits how often she buys.

Today on the floor

Tell a colleague in two sentences how Pay as you go and Gold compare for someone who wears glasses and buys a pair every two years.

About 3 minutes, with a colleague.

What your coach looks for

  • Both plans described in plain words
  • Does not tell them which to choose
  • Mentions all four plans exist
