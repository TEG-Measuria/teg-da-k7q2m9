Product objections: why coatings, do I need varifocals

7 minutes · The conversation

Purpose

You can answer two product questions: "why do I need coatings?" and "do I really need varifocals?". It matters because these are questions about understanding, not refusal, and a clear honest answer builds trust.

Worked example

Mrs Q, 57, bookkeeper, is told the optometrist advises varifocals and that the lens has a coating. She asks, "Why do I need all these extras? Aren't coatings just an add-on?"

The dispenser acknowledges: "That is a fair question, and I would ask it too." She clarifies: "What do you notice most with your current glasses?" Mrs Q says glare from headlights and smudges. The dispenser answers: "An anti-reflective coating cuts the reflections off the back and front of the lens, so there is less glare from headlights and screens, and it looks clearer to other people too. A hard, easy-clean layer is what keeps them from smudging. It is not an add-on to the lens, it is part of how the lens works for you." She checks: "Does that fit what you notice?" Mrs Q says yes, and the dispenser says the price of the pair with and without.

The lesson

Why coatings

Explain a coating through what the patient notices, not through what it is called. Common ones:

  • Anti-reflective: less glare from headlights and screens, a clearer view, and the lenses look more transparent. This is about comfort and clarity.
  • Hard coat and easy clean: resists scratches and smudges, and makes cleaning easier.
  • Light-reactive: lenses that darken outdoors and clear indoors, for everyday glare. They barely darken in a car.
  • Polarised: cuts glare from roads and water; best as a second pair for driving or the outdoors.

Blue light filters are about comfort only. Never make an eye-health claim about them.

If a patient asks "what happens if I leave it off?", answer honestly: they keep the glare, smudges and reflections. Do not scare them. If they still do not want it, accept that.

Do I need varifocals?

Whether a patient needs a varifocal depends on their prescription and on what the optometrist advised. Your job is to explain, in plain words, what it does and why the optometrist advises it.

  • Say what it does: "A varifocal lets you see far, near and in between with one pair, so you do not swap glasses."
  • Be honest about the adjustment: "Most people take a few days to get used to them. I will show you where to look, and if anything feels odd, come straight back and we will check them."
  • If the patient says "I do not need them", find out why. They may be using two pairs already, or they may be reluctant about the look or the adjustment.
  • If the optometrist did not advise a varifocal, do not offer it. If unsure what the optometrist advised, check the record or ask a registrant.

Whether any lens design is clinically appropriate is for the optometrist or dispensing optician, not for you. If the patient raises a clinical worry, such as difficulty with a previous pair, fetch a registrant.

Closing a product objection

Use the five steps: acknowledge, clarify, answer with the benefit to them, check, close. Keep the answer short, in their words, and end with the next step.

Watch-outs

  • Do not claim blue light filters protect eye health.
  • Do not tell a patient a lens is essential unless the optometrist has advised it.
  • Do not use technical coating names as the explanation.
  • Do not promise instant adaptation to varifocals.
  • If unsure whether a design suits a patient, ask a registrant.

Tips

  • Show the coating: tilt a lens with and without under the light.
  • Use the line "It is part of how the lens works for you."
  • With varifocals, put the patient in a trial frame or show the design chart if available in your practice.

Current practice notes

  • Coating benefits are restated from public manufacturer pages in plain words; use the product lessons in this course for names.
  • The Dispensing Conversation, Module 12, gives the first-time varifocal script.
  • Check with your practice manager: which trial frames or demonstration tools your practice holds.

Drill

A patient says, "Why do I need an anti-reflective coating?" Answer in two sentences using what they might notice.

Show model answer

"It cuts the reflections off the lens, so there is less glare from headlights and screens, and people can see your eyes more clearly. It also helps keep the lenses cleaner. Does the glare bother you at the moment?"

Today on the floor

A colleague asks why do I need coatings, then do I really need varifocals, using the product objection cards. Answer in plain, honest words with no jargon. Swap roles.

About 5 minutes, with a colleague.

What your coach looks for

  • Explains in terms of what they will notice
  • Honest: says when it is a personal choice
  • No medical claims
  • Passes clinical questions to a registrant
