Frame parts and the box measurements

7 minutes · Frames, styling and measuring

Purpose

You can name every part of a frame and read the three numbers printed on it. That lets you pick frames that suit the patient's face and prescription, and talk to a colleague or a lab without guessing.

Worked example

Mr D, 52, a plumber, brings back a frame he likes. The inside of the side says 52 18 140. You are asked: "Is it too wide for him?"

First you read the numbers. 52 is the eye size, the width of one lens opening, in millimetres. 18 is the bridge width, the gap between the two lenses. 140 is the side length. Add the eye size twice and the bridge: 52 + 52 + 18 = 122 mm. That is the width of the lens area from edge to edge.

Next you look at the patient. The frame front sits comfortably across his face without pinching at the temples. Then you check his pupils against the lens centres: his distance PD is 64 mm, and the frame's centre-to-centre distance is 52 + 18 = 70 mm. The difference is 6 mm, so each lens centre would sit 3 mm further out than his pupil unless the lab shifts the lens in (decentres it). For a low prescription that is fine. You tell your colleague: "Fits his face, but 6 mm of decentration in total, so I would not choose it for a stronger prescription."

The lesson

The parts of a frame

  • Front: the part that holds the lenses. It has two rims (the rings round the lenses) and a bridge.
  • Bridge: joins the two rims and rests on the nose, either on its own (a keyhole or saddle bridge) or through pads.
  • Pads: small cushions on metal frames that rest on the nose. They can be moved in and out.
  • End pieces: the parts at each outer edge of the front where the hinge is fixed.
  • Hinge: lets the side fold. Spring hinges flex outwards a little.
  • Sides (also called temples or arms): run back to the ears. The bent end is the earpiece or tip.
  • Rim groove: in a metal or nylon-cord frame, the groove the lens sits in.

The box measurements Almost every frame has numbers printed on the inside of a side, written like 52 18 140, sometimes with a small square between the first two (52 [] 18).

  • Eye size (A): the width of one lens opening, in millimetres.
  • Bridge (DBL): the distance between the lenses, also in millimetres.
  • Side length: the length of the side from hinge to tip.

The "box" is the imaginary rectangle drawn round one lens. Width A is across the box; B is the depth (height) of the box. B is not printed on the frame. You measure it with a ruler, and it matters for varifocals and for lens thickness.

Frame PD (centre-to-centre distance) is the eye size plus the bridge. It is not printed, you work it out. Compare it with the patient's PD. The nearer the two are, the less the lens has to be shifted from the middle of the blank to line up with the pupil.

Why the numbers matter

  • A frame much wider than the face looks too big and slides down. A frame much narrower pinches the temples.
  • A large eye size with a small PD means a lot of lens is cut away, so the lens is thicker at the edge in high minus. We cover this in c-08.
  • Shallow frames limit the near area of a varifocal. We come to that in c-11.

Watch-outs

  • Eye size alone does not tell you if a frame fits. Check the bridge and side length too.
  • Brand sizing varies. A 52 in one brand can look bigger than a 52 in another because of rim thickness and shape.
  • The numbers are in millimetres. Do not confuse them with inches or with the PD.
  • If the printed numbers are missing or worn, measure with a ruler and do not guess.

Tips

  • Say the three numbers out loud as a habit: "52, 18, 140".
  • Keep a small ruler in your pocket. Measure the B depth whenever a varifocal is going in.
  • Learn the sizes of the frames you recommend most. You will quickly spot which will suit a small or a wide face.

Current practice notes

  • Frame sizes on a dispense are recorded as eye size, bridge and side length in Optix One (desktop) alongside the frame.
  • Check with your practice manager: where the box depth (B) and the frame's centre-to-centre distance are recorded for your practice's orders.

Drill

A frame is marked 50 20 145. Say out loud what each number is, and work out the frame PD. Then say whether it suits a patient with a PD of 62 mm and what you would check.

Show model answer

50 is the eye size (lens opening width), 20 is the bridge, 145 is the side length, all in millimetres. Frame PD = 50 + 20 = 70 mm. The patient's PD is 62 mm, so the lenses would need to be moved in by 8 mm in total, 4 mm each side. That is a lot of decentration. Fine for a low prescription, but check with a registrant before using it for a stronger one, and look for a frame with a smaller bridge instead.

Today on the floor

Find three frames on display and read the three printed numbers aloud. Say which is the eye size, the bridge and the temple, and work out the frame PD.

About 4 minutes, with a colleague.

What your coach looks for

  • Each number is named correctly
  • Frame PD worked out correctly for each
  • Points to the parts of the frame by name
