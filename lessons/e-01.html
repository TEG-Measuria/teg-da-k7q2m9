Optix: finding the patient and their prescription

6 minutes · Systems

Screen pictures coming soon. These steps come from our written guides. If your Optix One (desktop) screen looks different, follow your manager and tap Report a wrong step.

Purpose

After this lesson you can open the right patient in Optix One (desktop), find their latest prescription and check it is the one you should use. Everything else in a dispense depends on starting in the correct record.

Worked example

Mr J, 71, retired farmer, has come in for his new glasses. The patient list shows two people with the same surname.

  1. Ask him to confirm his full name and date of birth.
  2. Search in Optix One (desktop) and compare both details with the record. Pick the one that matches both.
  3. Open the patient's record. Check the name at the top before you do anything else.
  4. Find the most recent test. Check the date of the test and that it includes a prescription.
  5. Read the prescription out to yourself and check the numbers make sense against what he wears now.

If he had given only a surname, you could have billed the wrong person. Two details, always.

The lesson

Steps.

  1. Ask the patient for their full name and date of birth. Do not read them out for the patient to say "yes".
  2. Search for the patient in Optix One (desktop) using those details.
  3. Check the name and date of birth on the record match what the patient told you.
  4. Open the patient's record and look at the tests. Select the most recent test.
  5. Check the date. A prescription more than two years old is out of date for selling spectacles, so ask a registrant before you go any further.
  6. Check the prescription is complete: sphere, cylinder and axis for each eye, and an add if the patient needs near correction.
  7. Compare with their current glasses if you have them. If a number looks very different, ask a registrant. A big change can be right, but check.
  8. Note the patient's PDs only after you have measured them. Do not copy them from an old record.

You never change a prescription. If you think it is wrong, or the test is missing or incomplete, ask the optometrist or a dispensing optician.

Check you are in the correct record before billing. The payment steps assume you are in the right patient record. If you take payment in the wrong record, the receipt and the history are wrong.

Patient data. Do not leave a record open on a screen where other patients can see it. Close it when you have finished.

These are screen steps from 2024 documents. The screens in Optix One (desktop) may differ. Check with your practice manager: the exact patient search screen and the test list in your version.

Watch-outs

  • Choosing the right surname but the wrong person.
  • Using an old test when a newer one exists.
  • Selling against a prescription that is out of date without asking a registrant.
  • Editing a prescription. You never do this.
  • Leaving a patient record open on a shared screen.

Tips

  • Ask for two identifiers every time.
  • Read the date of the test aloud as part of the check.
  • If the record looks odd, stop and ask rather than carrying on.

Current practice notes

  • Steps are taken from 2024 SOP documents and are not yet checked on the live screen.
  • Adult spectacles may be sold against a prescription under two years old; the exemption needs the focimeter and centring checks.
  • Check with your practice manager: the exact patient search and test-selection screens in Optix One (desktop).

Drill

Write the two things you ask a patient before you search, and the three checks you make on the test you select.

Show model answer

full name and date of birth. Checks: it is the most recent test, its date is within two years, and the prescription is complete for both eyes (and has an add if needed). If any check fails, ask a registrant.

Today on the floor

With your practice manager beside you, open the practice system on a training or test record if there is one (training or test record only: never a named patient's live record). Say aloud the steps to find a patient and their latest test, then point to where the test date and the sphere, cylinder, axis and add are shown. Do not write down any patient details.

About 4 minutes, with your coach.

What your coach looks for

  • Asks for two identifiers (name plus date of birth) without reading them out for the patient
  • Points to the date of the most recent test and says what to do if it is over two years old
  • Names the point at which they would stop and ask a registrant
  • Closes the record afterwards
