Frame materials and nickel allergy

7 minutes · Frames, styling and measuring

Purpose

You can describe the main frame materials in plain words, match them to a patient's lifestyle and skin, and spot a possible nickel allergy before it becomes a sore patient and a remake.

Worked example

Mrs P, 44, a nurse, says her last metal frame left a red, itchy mark on her nose and behind her ears. She also says her earrings sometimes do the same. You do not say "it's probably the pads". You ask: "Have you ever been told you react to nickel or cheap jewellery?" She says yes.

You show her three frames: a plastic (acetate) frame, a titanium frame and a stainless steel frame marked as nickel-free. You explain the difference in a sentence each: "Acetate has no metal where it touches your skin. Titanium is a metal that most people with a nickel allergy tolerate. Ordinary metal frames can contain nickel in the alloy." She chooses the titanium frame in a soft colour. You note on the record that she reports a nickel sensitivity, and you tell her: "If you get any redness, come and see us and we will change it."

The lesson

The main materials

  • Acetate (cellulose acetate): a plastic made from plant fibre. It comes in lots of colours and patterns, is warm to the touch and can be gently reshaped with heat. It is the usual choice for bold or classic looks and for anyone sensitive to metal.
  • Propionate and injected plastics (TR-90, nylon): light and flexible. Popular for children and sport. They do not heat-adjust like acetate and may need to be replaced rather than reshaped.
  • Stainless steel: strong and thin. Some contain nickel.
  • Monel and other nickel alloys: cheaper metals often plated with colour. The plating can wear through.
  • Titanium: light, strong and hypoallergenic. It is more expensive to make and often thin and quiet in style.
  • Beta titanium: a flexible titanium alloy that springs back to shape. It is used in lightweight frames.
  • Memory metal (flexon-type): springs back to shape after being bent.
  • Wood, horn, carbon fibre: special materials. Ask a colleague before adjusting.

Nickel allergy Nickel is a metal found in many alloys. In some people, skin that touches it develops a rash that can itch and blister. In glasses, the places that touch the skin are the nose pads, the bridge and the earpieces, so these are where a reaction appears.

  • Ask the patient at the dispense. Good questions: "Do earrings, watches or belts ever irritate your skin?" and "Have you been told you are allergic to anything metal?"
  • If yes, offer acetate or titanium. Check that any metal parts touching the skin (pad arms, rim wire, hinge) are labelled nickel-free.
  • Note it on the patient record and the job.
  • A reaction to an existing pair is a reason to change the material, not to put up with it. A rash that is spreading or very sore is a reason to see a clinician. It is not your decision to diagnose it.

Other points

  • Lens edge and nose pads: silicone pads are softer than hard plastic pads and kinder on sensitive skin.
  • Plating can wear off with sweat, hand cream or skin acids, leaving a different metal exposed.
  • Weight matters: heavier materials mean more pressure on the nose.

Watch-outs

  • Do not say a frame is "hypoallergenic" unless the label or the supplier says so. If you do not know, say so and ask.
  • Do not diagnose a rash. Say what you can do for the frame and suggest they speak to a pharmacist or GP about the skin.
  • Never promise "no reaction". Say you will change the frame if one occurs.
  • If unsure, ask a registrant.

Tips

  • Ask the nickel question early, before the patient falls for a frame.
  • Keep a few titanium and acetate frames in a range of colours at the front of the display.
  • Teach patients to wipe pads and earpieces with a soft cloth: sweat and make-up build up there.

Current practice notes

  • Frame ranges differ by practice and supplier. Check the supplier's catalogue or ask a colleague which frames are titanium or nickel-free.
  • Check with your practice manager: how a nickel allergy should be recorded in Optix One (desktop) for your practice.

Drill

Mr H, 30, an electrician, wants a thin metal frame. You ask the nickel question and he says his watch strap always gives him a rash. Say out loud what you would offer, what you would check and what you would record.

Show model answer

Offer a titanium frame or an acetate frame. Check that any metal that touches the skin (pads, pad arms, rim wire, hinge) is labelled nickel-free. Record that he reports a nickel sensitivity on the patient record and on the job, and tell him that if he sees redness you will change the material. Do not diagnose any rash. If unsure, ask a registrant.

Today on the floor

Find one metal and one acetate frame on display. Ask a colleague the nickel question and say what you offer if they say yes.

About 3 minutes, with a colleague.

What your coach looks for

  • Alternative materials named
