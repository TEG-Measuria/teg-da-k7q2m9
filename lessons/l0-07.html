Optical words you will hear on day one

6 minutes · Induction

Purpose

After this lesson you can follow what colleagues say on day one and explain the words in plain English. It matters because patients ask what everything means, and you will feel far more confident when you can answer.

Worked example

On her first morning, a new starter overhears: "Mrs J is a medium varifocal, add plus two, PD sixty-two, we will want a thin index. Remember to focimeter the old pair and book the follow-up." It sounds like a foreign language. By the end of this lesson each phrase has a meaning: a varifocal is a lens for far, middle and near vision; the add is the extra power for reading; PD is the distance between the pupils; index is the lens material; a focimeter is the instrument that measures a lens; and the follow-up is the check-in after collection.

The lesson

People and rules

  • Registrant: someone on the General Optical Council (GOC) register, either an optometrist or a dispensing optician.
  • Optometrist: tests eyes, checks eye health and writes the prescription.
  • Dispensing optician (DO): advises on, fits and supplies glasses; is always a registrant (GOC-registered).
  • Prescription (Rx): the written power of the lenses a patient needs, given after a sight test.

On the prescription

  • Dioptre (D): the unit that measures lens power.
  • Sphere: the main power, plus for long sight and minus for short sight.
  • Cylinder and axis: extra power, and its direction, used to correct astigmatism.
  • Add: the extra power added for reading.
  • Prism: a power that shifts the image to help the eyes work together.

Lens types

  • Single vision (SV): one power over the whole lens.
  • Bifocal: two powers, distance and reading, with a visible dividing line.
  • Varifocal (progressive): a smooth change in power from far to near, with no visible line.
  • Office or occupational lens: a lens for desk and screen work.
  • Index: how strongly a lens material bends light. A higher index lets the lens be thinner for the same prescription.
  • Coating: a surface treatment, such as anti-reflection or scratch protection.
  • Tint and photochromic: lenses that are coloured, or that darken in sunlight.

Measuring and checking

  • PD (pupillary distance): the distance between the pupils.
  • Height: how high above the frame bottom the eye sits. It matters most for varifocals.
  • Focimeter (lensometer): the instrument that checks the power and centre of a lens.
  • Vertex distance: the gap between the back of the lens and the eye.

Around the counter

  • Dispense: choosing and ordering glasses with a patient.
  • Collection: the visit where the finished glasses are checked and handed over.
  • Reglaze: new lenses in an existing frame.
  • Recall: a reminder that the next eye test is due.
  • NHS voucher: help towards glasses for people who qualify.

Watch-outs

  • Do not use the jargon with patients. Say "the extra power for reading", not "the add".
  • Do not guess a word's meaning from the sound. "Vertex" is not "vertical".
  • If unsure about a word, ask. Nobody expects you to know them all.

Tips

  • Make your own page of ten words you hear on day one and add one a day.
  • Try explaining each word to a friend as if they were a patient.

Current practice notes

  • Check with your practice manager: any abbreviations that are specific to your practice or the Optix screens.

Drill

Explain in one plain sentence each: add, PD, focimeter, index.

Show model answer

Add: the extra power for reading. PD: the distance between the pupils. Focimeter: the instrument that checks the power and centre of a lens. Index: how strongly the lens material bends light; higher index can be thinner for the same prescription.

Today on the floor

Ask a colleague to point to a focimeter and a PD ruler on the floor. Explain add, PD, focimeter and index to them in one plain sentence each.

About 4 minutes, with a colleague.

What your coach looks for

  • Each explanation is in everyday words
  • Colleague could repeat it back
  • Correctly points out the two instruments
