Selling the varifocal: show the difference

7 minutes · Varifocal specialist

Purpose

You can help a patient choose a varifocal by showing the difference, so they pick what suits them and feel confident, without pressure.

Worked example

Mr E, 57, bus driver, needs varifocals. You ask, "Tell me about a typical day." He drives, reads the paper and uses his phone. You say, "Let me show you something." You hold up a demonstration of the entry lens and the top lens, and ask him to look through each at a sign across the room and then at a card in his hand. "Which do you find easier?" He says the second feels wider and calmer. You say, "That is the extra personalisation. It is not for everyone, but you spend a lot of time looking at a distance, so you may notice it." You show him the price on Price-it and say, "It is your choice. The entry lens is a good lens too." He chooses the second. He feels he decided, not that he was sold.

The lesson

Selling a varifocal is about showing, not telling. A patient who has seen the difference makes a better choice and is happier later.

The steps

  1. Ask. How do they spend their day? What do they find difficult now?
  2. Show. Use demonstration lenses or a card. Let them look through two steps of the ladder at the same target.
  3. Explain in outcomes. Wider clear areas, a calmer feel, easier adaptation. Not jargon.
  4. Offer the ladder. Describe the steps and let the patient choose. Use Price-it for the price.
  5. Mention the extras that fit. A second pair for the computer or for sun, if it suits their life.
  6. Reassure. Tell them how you will help them adapt: the follow-up call at a week.

What to avoid

  • Pushing the top step to everyone.
  • Making medical claims. Talk about comfort and use.
  • Typing or guessing a price, or overriding one in Optix One (desktop).
  • Putting down the lower steps. They are good lenses.

Handling "it is too expensive". Acknowledge it. Show the next step down and say what they would notice. Ask what matters most to them. Never invent a discount; use only the codes your practice has. The member plans may help; check the plan the patient is on.

Handling "I tried varifocals and hated them". Ask what went wrong. It may have been the measurements, the frame or the design. Do not dismiss it; involve a registrant if there is a history of difficulty.

After the choice. Explain the measuring process: you measure carefully because it matters for the lens. Patients trust a dispenser who takes care.

Practice-agnostic. The Dispensing Conversation gives the full approach; this lesson links to it rather than copying it.

Watch-outs

  • Do not oversell. A lens that is more than the patient needs loses trust.
  • Do not criticise the entry lens.
  • Do not apply a discount that is not on the code table.
  • Do not make health claims.

Tips

  • Let the patient hold the demonstration lens.
  • Ask "which is easier?" rather than "which is better?".
  • Keep the demo lenses clean.

Drill

Role-play with a colleague: show two steps of the ladder to a patient who says "I just want the cheapest".

Show model answer

Acknowledge the wish. Ask about their day, let them look through the entry lens and one step up at the same target, ask which is easier, then give the price from the price list and leave the choice to them. No pressure, no invented discounts.

Today on the floor

Role-play selling a varifocal with a colleague as the patient, using the six steps: ask, show with demonstration lenses, explain in outcomes, offer the ladder, mention fitting extras, reassure. Swap roles. Prices come only from price-it.

About 5 minutes, with a colleague.

What your coach looks for

  • Shows rather than tells
  • Explains in outcomes, no jargon
  • Lets the patient choose; does not push the top step
  • Reassures with the follow-up call at about a week
