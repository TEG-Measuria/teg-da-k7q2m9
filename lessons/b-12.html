Transitions and photochromic lenses

7 minutes · Lenses

Purpose

After this lesson you can explain how Transitions lenses work, what they do and do not do, and when a clear lens plus sunglasses is the better answer. It matters because Transitions is the most common upgrade we offer, and many complaints come from a patient who expected it to behave like sunglasses.

Worked example

Mrs K, 61, a retired teacher, is choosing lenses for varifocals. She walks the dog every day and drives to the shops. She asks, "If I get the ones that go dark, do I need sunglasses?"

You answer in three parts. First, how it works: "The lens has a clear look indoors and goes darker outside when it meets daylight, then clears again when you come back in." Second, what it does well: "It saves swapping glasses on the dog walk and cuts glare on bright days." Third, where it falls short: "In the car it will hardly darken, because the windscreen blocks the light that triggers it, and it takes a little time to go dark and to clear. For driving in bright sun, a separate pair of sunglasses is better."

She decides: Transitions for the everyday pair and a pair of prescription sunglasses for driving. You check price-it for the combination, and write on the dispense what you have told her about driving.

The lesson

Photochromic means the lens changes in response to light. Transitions is the best-known make. In sunlight the lens darkens, in the shade and indoors it lightens. The change is triggered mainly by the UV in daylight, not by heat.

What affects the performance:

  • UV. Without UV, no darkening. A car windscreen blocks much of the UV, so a standard Transitions lens stays fairly light inside a car. Essilor offers some versions made to react more behind a windscreen; ask in practice for what is offered and what it costs.
  • Temperature. In cold weather the lens usually goes darker and takes longer to clear; in hot weather it is lighter.
  • Time. The lens darkens in under a minute and takes longer to clear. Tell the patient so they are not surprised.

What Transitions does well: it gives one pair for indoors and outdoors, reduces glare on bright days and protects against UV. The UV protection comes from the lens itself, and clear lenses may also give UV protection; ask in practice for the lens in question rather than promising.

What it does not do: it is not a sunglass. It is not dark enough for very bright sun, and it is not suitable on its own for driving in bright sun. It will not darken behind most windscreens. Use "may" and "many people find" in your advice.

Clear or Transitions on our price list. Each lens is priced clear and Transitions, but not every combination exists. For example, the Eyezen Start Transitions in the thinnest band and the E-reader (Immersia MID) Transitions in the lower bands are not offered. For an offered combination, show both: at the standard index the Road Pilot clear pair is £250 and the Transitions pair is £310. Use Price-it for your actual combination.

How to recommend. Ask about their day: do they go in and out? Do they drive in bright sun? Do they work outdoors? Do they dislike changing glasses? If they are mostly indoors or in the car, Transitions has less to offer. A good pattern: Transitions for the everyday pair, plus a polarised sun pair if they drive (b-13).

Watch-outs

  • Do not say Transitions lenses are sunglasses.
  • Do not promise they will darken in the car.
  • Do not promise a specific speed of darkening; say "under a minute" and "clearing takes longer".
  • Do not offer a Transitions combination before checking price-it.
  • If unsure, ask a colleague or the practice manager.

Tips

  • Show the patient a demonstration lens in daylight or under a UV torch.
  • Ask "do you drive in bright sun?" before you recommend.
  • Write on the dispense what the patient was told about driving and sun.

Current practice notes

  • Transitions is offered as a separate column on the group price list; some combinations are not available, so check price-it.
  • Essilor publishes the available Transitions types and their behaviour; use their guide for specifics.
  • Extra-dark and behind-the-windscreen versions are listed as "ask in practice".

Drill

"Mr A, 40, a postman, works outdoors all day and drives a van." Say in 30 seconds what you would offer, and what you would warn him about.

Show model answer

Transitions for his everyday pair, because he moves between indoors and outdoors and it saves swapping. Warn him that it will stay fairly light in the van because the windscreen blocks the UV that triggers it, and offer a polarised prescription sun pair for driving in bright sun. Check price-it for the combination before quoting.

Today on the floor

Explain Transitions to a colleague who works outdoors and drives a van. 30 seconds, including what to warn them about.

About 3 minutes, with a colleague.

What your coach looks for

  • Suggests clear lens plus sunglasses where that is better
  • No overselling
