Crizal coatings: what the patient actually gets

7 minutes · Lenses

Purpose

After this lesson you can explain what a lens coating does in everyday words, say what is included with each lens on our price list, and answer "is it worth it?" honestly. It matters because the coating is the part of the lens the patient notices every day: reflections, smears and scratches.

Worked example

Mr Z, 52, a bank manager, has been quoted for a pair of single vision lenses and asks, "What is this coating and do I need it? My last pair had one and it still got scratched."

You ask what he noticed with the old pair. He says reflections in the lenses when he is on video calls, and smears he could not clean. You explain: "A coating is a very thin layer on the lens. The anti-reflection layer cuts the reflections, so the lens looks clearer to you and people see your eyes in photos and on calls. A hard layer helps against everyday scratches, though no lens is scratch-proof. On our list the lenses include a good coating, and the premium ones come with Crizal UV, which also deals with UV reflecting off the back of the lens into your eye." You show him the difference with a demonstration pair: one with and one without.

He asks whether it will stop scratches. You say honestly that it helps, that nothing is scratch-proof, and that how he cleans the lenses matters. You show him how to clean them with a microfibre cloth and lens spray. He is happy to proceed.

The lesson

A lens coating is a set of very thin layers added to the lens surfaces. In plain words, there are four jobs:

  1. Anti-reflection. Plain lenses reflect some light. That makes the lens look shiny, can cause ghost images and glare at night, and hides the eyes. An anti-reflection coating reduces this, so the lens looks clearer and the wearer's eyes can be seen.
  2. Hard (scratch resistance). A hard layer protects the soft lens material from everyday wear.
  3. Easy to clean. Layers that repel water and oil make smears and dust easier to wipe off.
  4. UV. Some coatings also reduce UV reflected from the back surface towards the eye.

Crizal is Essilor's family of coatings. The ones you will see on our price list are shown as Crizal UV on the premium lens rows and as HMC (hard multi-coat) on single vision, bifocal and some varifocals. Essilor publishes the full Crizal range with different strengths, such as extra scratch resistance or extra help with glare; if a patient asks about a specific coating, ask in practice before promising it.

What the patient actually gets (say this in plain words):

  • clearer, cleaner-looking lenses with fewer reflections;
  • more comfort at night and on screens, where reflections are most obvious;
  • lenses that are easier to clean;
  • better looks for others, because they see the eyes, not the reflection.

What to avoid saying:

  • "Scratch-proof." No lens is. Say "more resistant to everyday scratches".
  • "It protects your eyes from UV." The honest statement is that it reduces UV reflected from the back surface of the lens, and that the lens material also affects UV protection. Keep to comfort and ask in practice if a patient wants more.
  • Any claim about health.

Care. Wash the lenses with lukewarm water, use lens spray and a clean microfibre cloth, and avoid wiping dry dust off with clothing or tissues. Heat, such as leaving glasses on a car dashboard, can damage a coating.

The coating and the price list. The coating comes with the lens on our list; you do not usually sell it as a separate extra. For example, the Eyezen Start clear pair at the standard index is £235 and includes Crizal UV. For anything that is not on the list, ask in practice.

Watch-outs

  • Do not say "scratch-proof".
  • Do not make health claims about UV or glare.
  • Do not promise a specific Crizal name or feature unless you have checked it.
  • Do not tell a patient to clean lenses on a dry cloth or clothing.
  • If unsure, ask a colleague or the practice manager.

Tips

  • Show the difference with and without a coating on a demonstration pair.
  • Give every patient a cloth and a care card.
  • Say "may" for effects and "will" only for things that are certain.

Current practice notes

  • On the price list, premium lenses are shown with Crizal UV and single vision, bifocal and some varifocals with HMC.
  • Essilor publishes the full Crizal range and features; use their product guide for specifics.
  • Whether a different Crizal coating can be ordered for a patient and at what extra cost: Check with your practice manager: the current coating options and prices.

Drill

A "Mrs O, 66" asks, "Will this coating make my glasses scratch-proof?" Answer in two sentences, and say how she should clean them.

Show model answer

"It makes them more resistant to everyday scratches, though no lens is scratch-proof. Clean them with lukewarm water, a drop of lens spray and a clean microfibre cloth, and please do not leave them on a hot dashboard."

Today on the floor

Using the printed price list, find which coating comes with a lens. Answer 'will it be scratch-proof?' in two sentences and say how to clean glasses.

About 3 minutes, with a colleague.
