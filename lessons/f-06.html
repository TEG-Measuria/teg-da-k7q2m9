Remakes and warranty: triage before you promise

8 minutes · Aftercare

Purpose

You can sort a remake or warranty request into the right group before saying anything to the patient, so you never promise what the practice cannot deliver.

Worked example

Mrs G, 62, retired nurse, returns with her varifocals after five weeks. "The coating is peeling at the edge." You do not say "we will remake those". You say, "I am sorry about that. Let me look at it properly." You see small crazing at the edge of one lens. You open her job in Optix One (desktop), note the lens and coating, and check whether the lens was eyecoded and who supplied it. You take the details and tell her that you will find out what the supplier covers and ring her back.

In the back office you find the product is a coating the supplier guarantees. You complete the supplier's guarantee claim form for the product concerned, scan the return form into her record, add the return to the tracker, and later check that the credit note arrives.

The lesson

Triage means sorting before acting. For any remake or warranty request, put it into one of these groups first.

  1. Practice or lab error. The glasses were not made as ordered. The remake is usually the practice or supplier's cost.
  2. Coating or material fault. The coating crazes or peels, the lens delaminates. This may be a supplier guarantee claim. Check the correct claim form for the product (for Crizal there are separate forms for different products).
  3. Damage. Dropped, bent, chewed, left in a hot car. Usually not covered; goodwill is a separate matter.
  4. Prescription changed. A new prescription from a new test means a new job, not a remake.
  5. Non-adaptation. The patient cannot get on with the glasses. A dispensing optician must be involved before lenses are returned.

Before you say anything: take the details, identify the group, check the paperwork, and speak to a dispensing optician or the practice manager. Never say yes or no to a refund on the spot. The owner signs off refunds.

The paperwork: scan a copy of the return form into the patient's record to avoid repeat remakes. Update the tracker with the date, return details and recipient. Check that supplier credit notes arrive, and chase if they are overdue. Double-check the tracker entries.

Warranty wording. A 2024 email said the supplier's one-year return guarantee applies only to eyecoded lenses. We treat that as the reason to eyecode every eligible lens, but the exact wording is Check with your manager and must not be quoted to a patient as fact.

What to say to the patient: "I am sorry. I will find out what we can do and ring you by tomorrow." Then do it.

Watch-outs

  • Do not promise a free remake before you know the group.
  • Do not say yes or no to a refund on the spot.
  • Do not forget the tracker and the credit note; unrecorded returns are lost money.
  • Do not quote the warranty wording to a patient until confirmed.

Tips

  • Make a one-page triage card with the five groups.
  • Photograph visible damage or coating faults and attach them to the record.
  • Ring back when you said you would.

Current practice notes

  • A Crizal return follows the Returns process: assess, scan the return form to the patient record, update the tracker, cancel and re-order.
  • The 2024 Crizal claim forms need checking for current versions.
  • Check with your manager the current warranty wording and the one-year return guarantee on eyecoded lenses. Check with your practice manager.
  • Refunds are signed off by the owner.

Drill

A patient's glasses have deep scratches and a bent frame and she asks for a free replacement. Which group is this and what do you say?

Show model answer

Damage (group 3). Do not say yes or no. Say you are sorry, take details and say you will find out what the practice can do and ring back. Pass it to the dispensing optician or practice manager. Goodwill is decided by them.

Today on the floor

Your coach reads out three requests (a coating that peeled, glasses sat on and bent, a new prescription from a new test). Put each in the right triage group and say what you would tell the patient. Then ask your manager who decides on a free remake at your practice.

About 4 minutes, with your coach.

What your coach looks for

  • Sorts each request into the right group before speaking to the patient
  • Uses the line: I will find out and ring you by tomorrow
  • Says a dispensing optician or the manager must be involved first
  • Does not say yes or no to a refund
